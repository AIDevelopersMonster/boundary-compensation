## Abstract

Article I showed that the exact contextual transport attached to unitary operator reorderings is flat, whereas pair-only context reduction can generate nontrivial discrete holonomy. The present work asks for an open-system analogue. The key mechanism is not nonunitarity by itself: a finite-time Lindblad propagator may remain invertible as a linear superoperator even though its inverse is not physically admissible. Instead we study the failure of a unital completely positive reduction to preserve multiplicative context. For a UCP map `Phi` we introduce the multiplicativity defect `Delta_Phi(X,Y)=Phi(XY)-Phi(X)Phi(Y)` and derive a norm bound controlling the reduced loop holonomy of an exactly flat contextual loop. Flatness is preserved on the multiplicative domain of `Phi`, and Stinespring dilation expresses the defect through leakage out of the represented system subspace. For uniformly continuous Lindblad dynamics we identify the infinitesimal Leibniz defect and derive a first-order reduced-loop curvature formula. The resulting framework separates algebraic reversibility, physical complete positivity, context loss, and path dependence. Partial-context hierarchies, channel examples, monotonicity questions, and unbounded-generator extensions are left as explicit programme obligations.

## 1. From exact contextual flatness to physical reduction

Let `A` and `B` be unital C*-algebras and let `Phi: A -> B` be a unital completely positive map. Consider unitary contextual edge transports `T_1,...,T_m in A` satisfying the exact loop identity

`T_m ... T_1 = I`.

Article I would assign identity holonomy to this full contextual loop. After reduction define

`H_Phi := Phi(T_m) ... Phi(T_1)`.

The map `Phi` is generally not multiplicative, so `H_Phi` need not equal `Phi(I)=I`.

### Definition 1.1 — multiplicativity defect

For `X,Y in A`, define

`Delta_Phi(X,Y) := Phi(XY) - Phi(X)Phi(Y)`.

This is zero identically exactly when `Phi` is a unital *-homomorphism; more locally it vanishes on the multiplicative domain.

## 2. Reduced-loop defect bound

For `k >= 1` put `P_k := T_k ... T_1` and `Q_k := Phi(T_k) ... Phi(T_1)`.

### Theorem 2.1 — UCP defect bound

If `Phi` is UCP and the `T_k` are unitary, then

`||H_Phi - I|| <= sum_{k=2}^m ||Delta_Phi(T_k, P_{k-1})||`.

#### Proof

Set `E_k := Q_k - Phi(P_k)`. Since `E_1=0`,

`E_k = Phi(T_k)E_{k-1} - Delta_Phi(T_k,P_{k-1})`.

A UCP map is contractive, and `||T_k||=1`, hence `||Phi(T_k)||<=1`. Therefore

`||E_k|| <= ||E_{k-1}|| + ||Delta_Phi(T_k,P_{k-1})||`.

Iteration gives the claimed bound. Because `P_m=I` and `Phi(I)=I`, `E_m=H_Phi-I`. QED.

### Corollary 2.2 — multiplicative-domain flatness

If the C*-algebra generated by `T_1,...,T_m` is contained in the multiplicative domain `MD(Phi)`, then `H_Phi=I`.

Thus reduced curvature is not forced by dissipation or nonunitarity alone. It appears only when the physical reduction loses multiplicative context on the transport algebra relevant to the loop.

## 3. Stinespring form of the lost context

Let `Phi(X)=V^* pi(X) V` be a Stinespring representation with `V` an isometry and `P=VV^*`.

### Proposition 3.1 — Stinespring defect identity

`Delta_Phi(X,Y) = V^* pi(X) (I-P) pi(Y) V`.

#### Proof

Use `pi(XY)=pi(X)pi(Y)` and insert `I=P+(I-P)` between the two represented factors. The `P` term is exactly `Phi(X)Phi(Y)`. QED.

This identifies multiplicativity loss with an excursion through the complement of the represented system subspace in a chosen dilation. Any invariant interpretation must depend only on the channel, not on arbitrary dilation coordinates; this is an explicit later proof obligation.

## 4. Lindblad Leibniz defect

Let `Phi_t=e^{tL}` be a uniformly continuous unital quantum dynamical semigroup with bounded GKSL generator

`L(X)=i[H,X] + sum_alpha ( V_alpha^* X V_alpha - 1/2 {V_alpha^* V_alpha, X} )`.

Define

`Gamma_L(X,Y) := L(XY)-L(X)Y-X L(Y)`.

### Theorem 4.1 — GKSL Leibniz defect

`Gamma_L(X,Y) = sum_alpha [V_alpha^*,X][Y,V_alpha]`.

#### Proof

The Hamiltonian part `i[H, . ]` is a derivation and cancels from `Gamma_L`. Expanding one dissipator gives

`V^*XYV - V^*XVY - XV^*YV + XV^*VY`,

which factors as `[V^*,X][Y,V]`. Summing over `alpha` gives the result. QED.

The identity cleanly separates Hamiltonian contextual compatibility from dissipative failure of the Leibniz rule.

## 5. First-order reduced Lindblad holonomy

For the same exact contextual loop `T_m...T_1=I`, define

`H_t := Phi_t(T_m) ... Phi_t(T_1)`.

### Theorem 5.1 — first-order Lindblad order curvature

With `P_{k-1}=T_{k-1}...T_1`,

`H_t - I = -t sum_{k=2}^m T_m...T_{k+1} Gamma_L(T_k,P_{k-1}) + O(t^2)`

in operator norm as `t -> 0`.

#### Proof

Let `Q_k(t)=Phi_t(T_k)...Phi_t(T_1)` and `E_k(t)=Q_k(t)-Phi_t(P_k)`. Since `Phi_t=I+tL+O(t^2)` in operator norm and `E_1'(0)=0`, differentiation of the recursion gives

`E_k'(0)=T_k E_{k-1}'(0)-Gamma_L(T_k,P_{k-1})`.

Iterating yields

`E_m'(0)=-sum_{k=2}^m T_m...T_{k+1} Gamma_L(T_k,P_{k-1})`.

Since `P_m=I` and the semigroup is unital, `Phi_t(P_m)=I`; hence `E_m(t)=H_t-I`. QED.

### Interpretation boundary

This theorem does **not** identify the coefficient with a standard decoherence monotone, entropy production, or physical gauge curvature. It is a reduced order-holonomy coefficient measuring failure of the CP evolution to preserve the exact multiplicative context of the chosen loop.

## 6. Partial-context hierarchy

The next structural object is a chain of reductions

`A_full -> A_1 -> ... -> A_obs`.

Each level defines its own reduced loop product and hence a context-resolution profile. The main open question is to classify the minimal retained context required for path independence.

Universal monotonicity of curvature magnitude under further reductions is **not assumed**. Complete positivity can create a multiplicativity defect relative to one description while subsequent contraction may reduce its norm.

## 7. Numerical programme

The first exact test family should contain:

1. qubit dephasing;
2. amplitude damping in the Heisenberg picture;
3. depolarizing channels;
4. two Stinespring realizations of the same channel;
5. nested channels with different multiplicative domains.

For each example compare exact contextual flatness upstairs, channel-reduced holonomy, the defect-bound certificate, the first-order Lindblad coefficient, and stability under edge perturbations.

## 8. Open analytical problems

1. infinite-dimensional extension with unbounded generators;
2. characterization of flatness through channel multiplicative domains for generated transport algebras;
3. dilation-independent invariants of context loss;
4. conditions for monotonicity or controlled non-monotonicity under nested reductions;
5. relation, if any, to recoverability, entropy production, or information-loss measures;
6. extension from Markovian semigroups to non-Markovian process tensors or memory kernels.

## 9. Claim firewall

The present programme does not claim that dissipation automatically produces curvature, that the reduced holonomy is a spacetime or gauge curvature, or that it is already an operational decoherence measure. The proved bounded results concern multiplicativity loss of a CP reduction applied to contextual operator-order transports.

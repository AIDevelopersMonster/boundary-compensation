# Context Reduction in Open Quantum Systems: Multiplicativity Defects, Lindblad Order Curvature, and Partial Operator Context

## Abstract

The first article in this research line established an exactly flat contextual transport for unitary operator reorderings and showed that pair-only reduction can create nontrivial discrete order holonomy. The present paper asks what happens under physically admissible open-system reductions. The mechanism is not nonunitarity by itself. Instead, the relevant obstruction is the failure of a unital completely positive (UCP) map to preserve products on the transport algebra.

For a UCP map `Phi` we use the standard multiplicativity defect

`Delta_Phi(X,Y) = Phi(XY) - Phi(X)Phi(Y)`

and apply it to exactly flat loops `T_m ... T_1 = I`. We prove an exact decomposition of the reduced loop defect into transported multiplicativity defects; the previously obtained norm estimate is an immediate corollary. We derive an exact composition law for defects under nested reductions, giving a layer-resolved context-loss identity. Two-edge backtracking loops identify the UCP Schwarz defects and characterize the multiplicative domain by vanishing of both orientations. In Stinespring form the backtracking defect is the squared leakage out of the represented system subspace.

For a uniformly continuous unital quantum Markov semigroup `Phi_t = exp(tL)` we derive an exact evolution equation and Duhamel formula for `Delta_t`. With the bounded GKSL generator this converts the first-order Lindblad expression into an exact integral representation of reduced loop holonomy. A fully explicit dephasing example is then computed on the six-edge contextual braid loop inherited from Article I.

The standard theories of multiplicative domains, Stinespring dilation, GKSL generators, and noncommutative carré du champ are used as infrastructure rather than novelty claims. The contribution is their coupling to an exactly flat operator-order connection and the resulting exact identities for context-reduced holonomy.

## 1. Problem and claim boundary

Let `A` and `B` be unital C*-algebras and let `Phi: A -> B` be UCP. Let

`T_1,...,T_m in A`

be unitary edge transports satisfying the exact loop relation

`T_m ... T_1 = I`.

The unreduced contextual connection is therefore flat. Define the reduced loop product

`H_Phi := Phi(T_m) ... Phi(T_1)`.

In general `H_Phi != I` because `Phi` need not preserve products.

This paper does **not** claim that every dissipative evolution produces nonzero holonomy, that the quantities below are spacetime or gauge curvature, or that they are already standard decoherence monotones. The term *reduced order holonomy* refers only to the path defect of the declared reduced operator-order transport.

## 2. Exact loop-defect calculus

### Definition 2.1 — multiplicativity defect

For `X,Y in A`, define

`Delta_Phi(X,Y) := Phi(XY) - Phi(X)Phi(Y)`.

This is standard operator-algebraic infrastructure. Choi's multiplicative-domain theorem characterizes the subalgebra on which a UCP map behaves multiplicatively.

Put

`P_k := T_k ... T_1`,

`Q_k := Phi(T_k) ... Phi(T_1)`.

### Theorem 2.2 — exact reduced-loop defect decomposition

For every unital linear map `Phi` for which the displayed products are defined,

`H_Phi - I = - sum_{k=2}^m Phi(T_m)...Phi(T_{k+1}) Delta_Phi(T_k,P_{k-1})`,

where the leading product is `I` when `k=m`.

#### Proof

Define `E_k := Q_k - Phi(P_k)`. Since `E_1=0`,

`E_k = Phi(T_k)E_{k-1} - Delta_Phi(T_k,P_{k-1})`.

Iterating this identity gives

`E_m = - sum_{k=2}^m Phi(T_m)...Phi(T_{k+1}) Delta_Phi(T_k,P_{k-1})`.

Because `P_m=I` and `Phi` is unital, `E_m=H_Phi-I`. QED.

### Corollary 2.3 — UCP norm certificate

If `Phi` is UCP and all `T_k` are unitary, then

`||H_Phi-I|| <= sum_{k=2}^m ||Delta_Phi(T_k,P_{k-1})||`.

Indeed `||Phi(T_j)||<=1` for every unitary `T_j`.

### Corollary 2.4 — multiplicative-domain flatness

If the C*-algebra generated by `T_1,...,T_m` is contained in the multiplicative domain `MD(Phi)`, then

`H_Phi=I`.

Hence nonzero reduced holonomy is a certificate that the relevant transport algebra is not wholly contained in `MD(Phi)`. The converse for a particular loop is false in general: a map may fail to be multiplicative on the generated algebra while the defects of that specific loop cancel.

## 3. Nested context reductions

The natural mathematical model of intermediate context is a chain

`A_0 --Phi_1--> A_1 --Phi_2--> ... --Phi_r--> A_r`.

### Theorem 3.1 — composition law for multiplicativity defects

For composable unital linear maps `Phi` and `Psi`,

`Delta_{Psi o Phi}(X,Y) = Psi(Delta_Phi(X,Y)) + Delta_Psi(Phi(X),Phi(Y))`.

#### Proof

Insert and subtract `Psi(Phi(X)Phi(Y))`:

`Psi(Phi(XY)) - Psi(Phi(X))Psi(Phi(Y))`

`= Psi(Phi(XY)-Phi(X)Phi(Y))`

`  + Psi(Phi(X)Phi(Y))-Psi(Phi(X))Psi(Phi(Y))`. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — layer-resolved defect identity

Let `Phi_[j:1] := Phi_j o ... o Phi_1` and let the empty downstream composition be the identity. Then

`Delta_{Phi_[r:1]}(X,Y)`

`= sum_{j=1}^r Phi_r o ... o Phi_{j+1} (`

`    Delta_{Phi_j}(Phi_[j-1:1](X), Phi_[j-1:1](Y)) )`.

This gives an exact decomposition of final multiplicativity loss into defects generated at each reduction layer and propagated through all downstream reductions.

### Consequence 3.3 — no naive scalar monotonicity theorem

For UCP maps each downstream map is contractive, but the layer-resolved identity contains both transported old defects and newly created defects. Therefore a universal monotonicity claim for the scalar norm `||Delta||` or for loop holonomy does not follow from complete positivity alone. Any data-processing theorem requires additional structure.

## 4. Backtracking holonomy and the multiplicative domain

The smallest flat loop is a backtrack. Let `U` be unitary and consider the two orientations

`U, U*` and `U*, U`.

Define

`D_R^Phi(U) := I - Phi(U)^* Phi(U)`,

`D_L^Phi(U) := I - Phi(U) Phi(U)^*`.

### Theorem 4.1 — UCP backtracking defects

For a UCP map `Phi`,

`D_R^Phi(U) = Delta_Phi(U*,U) >= 0`,

`D_L^Phi(U) = Delta_Phi(U,U*) >= 0`.

The associated reduced backtracking products are

`H_R = Phi(U)^*Phi(U) = I-D_R^Phi(U)`,

`H_L = Phi(U)Phi(U)^* = I-D_L^Phi(U)`.

#### Proof

Unitality gives `Phi(U*U)=Phi(UU*)=I`, and complete positivity implies the Schwarz inequalities

`Phi(U)^*Phi(U) <= Phi(U*U)=I`,

`Phi(U)Phi(U)^* <= Phi(UU*)=I`. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — two-sided multiplicative-domain test

A unitary `U` lies in `MD(Phi)` if and only if

`D_R^Phi(U)=D_L^Phi(U)=0`.

This is precisely the two Schwarz-equality characterization of the multiplicative domain specialized to a unitary.

## 5. Stinespring leakage formula

Let

`Phi(X)=V* pi(X) V`

be a Stinespring representation, with `V` an isometry and `P=VV*`.

### Proposition 5.1 — Stinespring multiplicativity defect

`Delta_Phi(X,Y) = V* pi(X)(I-P)pi(Y)V`.

#### Proof

Write

`Phi(XY)=V*pi(X)pi(Y)V`

and insert `I=P+(I-P)` between the represented factors. The `P` term is

`V*pi(X)VV*pi(Y)V = Phi(X)Phi(Y)`. QED.

### Corollary 5.2 — backtracking defect is squared dilation leakage

For unitary `U`,

`D_R^Phi(U)`

`= ((I-P)pi(U)V)^* ((I-P)pi(U)V)`,

and therefore

`||D_R^Phi(U)|| = ||(I-P)pi(U)V||^2`.

Thus the right backtracking holonomy has an exact dilation interpretation: it measures the squared operator-norm leakage of the represented action of `U` out of the embedded system subspace. Minimal Stinespring dilations are unique up to an environment unitary, so this norm is not a coordinate artifact of a chosen minimal dilation.

## 6. Uniformly continuous quantum Markov semigroups

From here we work in a bounded setting, for example `A=M_d(C)` or `B(H)` with a uniformly continuous unital CP semigroup

`Phi_t = exp(t L)`.

For fixed `X,Y`, define

`Delta_t(X,Y) := Phi_t(XY)-Phi_t(X)Phi_t(Y)`.

Define the Leibniz defect

`Gamma_L(X,Y) := L(XY)-L(X)Y-X L(Y)`.

### Theorem 6.1 — exact evolution equation for the semigroup defect

`d/dt Delta_t(X,Y)`

`= L(Delta_t(X,Y)) + Gamma_L(Phi_t(X),Phi_t(Y))`,

with `Delta_0(X,Y)=0`.

#### Proof

Differentiate the definition of `Delta_t`. Writing `A_t=Phi_t(X)` and `B_t=Phi_t(Y)`, use

`Phi_t(XY)=Delta_t+A_tB_t`

and linearity of `L` to obtain

`L(Delta_t)+L(A_tB_t)-L(A_t)B_t-A_tL(B_t)`. The last three terms are exactly `Gamma_L(A_t,B_t)`. QED.

### Corollary 6.2 — exact Duhamel representation

`Delta_t(X,Y)`

`= integral_0^t Phi_{t-s}( Gamma_L(Phi_s(X),Phi_s(Y)) ) ds`.

This is the variation-of-constants formula for Theorem 6.1.

## 7. GKSL form and carré-du-champ relation

Let the bounded Heisenberg-picture generator be

`L(X)=i[H,X] + sum_alpha ( V_alpha^* X V_alpha - 1/2 {V_alpha^*V_alpha,X} )`.

### Theorem 7.1 — exact GKSL Leibniz defect

`Gamma_L(X,Y) = sum_alpha [V_alpha^*,X][Y,V_alpha]`.

#### Proof

The Hamiltonian commutator is a derivation and cancels. For one dissipative operator `V`, direct expansion gives

`V^*XYV - V^*XVY - XV^*YV + XV^*VY`,

which is `[V^*,X][Y,V]`. QED.

For `Y=A` and `X=A*`,

`Gamma_L(A*,A)=sum_alpha [A,V_alpha]^*[A,V_alpha] >= 0`.

Thus, with our sign convention, `Gamma_L(A*,A)` is the positive Schwarz-defect production form. It is equivalent up to the conventional sign/factor choice to the standard noncommutative carré-du-champ object used in quantum Markov semigroup theory. We do not claim this identity as new.

### Corollary 7.2 — exact positive evolution of the Schwarz defect

Define

`D_t(A):=Phi_t(A*A)-Phi_t(A)^*Phi_t(A)`.

Then

`D_t(A) = integral_0^t Phi_{t-s}(`

`  sum_alpha [Phi_s(A),V_alpha]^*[Phi_s(A),V_alpha] ) ds >= 0`.

For unitary `U`, this is exactly the right backtracking defect

`D_t(U)=I-Phi_t(U)^*Phi_t(U)`.

## 8. Exact Lindblad representation of reduced loop holonomy

Let the original contextual loop satisfy `T_m...T_1=I` and define

`H_t := Phi_t(T_m)...Phi_t(T_1)`.

### Theorem 8.1 — exact integral formula for reduced loop holonomy

Let `P_{k-1}=T_{k-1}...T_1`. Then

`H_t-I = - sum_{k=2}^m Phi_t(T_m)...Phi_t(T_{k+1})`

`  * integral_0^t Phi_{t-s}(`

`      Gamma_L(Phi_s(T_k),Phi_s(P_{k-1})) ) ds`.

#### Proof

Insert Corollary 6.2 into the exact loop decomposition of Theorem 2.2 with `Phi=Phi_t`. QED.

### Corollary 8.2 — first-order Lindblad order curvature

As `t -> 0`,

`H_t-I = -t sum_{k=2}^m T_m...T_{k+1}`

`  Gamma_L(T_k,P_{k-1}) + O(t^2)`

in operator norm.

This is now a linearization of the exact integral theorem rather than the primary result.

## 9. Exact channel examples

### 9.1 Backtracking examples

Let `U=X` be the Pauli `X` matrix.

**Dephasing.** If

`Phi_eta(X)=eta X`, `Phi_eta(Y)=eta Y`, `Phi_eta(Z)=Z`,

then

`H_R = eta^2 I`,

`||H_R-I||=1-eta^2`.

For the Markov semigroup `eta=e^{-gamma t}` this becomes

`||H_t-I||=1-e^{-2 gamma t}`.

**Qubit depolarization.** For

`Phi_eta(A)=eta A+(1-eta) tr(A) I/2`,

one again has `Phi_eta(X)=eta X`, hence the same backtracking law `eta^2 I`.

**Amplitude damping, Heisenberg picture.** With survival parameter `q in [0,1]`,

`Phi_q(X)=sqrt(q) X`,

so

`H_R=q I`,

`||H_R-I||=1-q`.

For the standard semigroup `q=e^{-gamma t}`, the defect is `1-e^{-gamma t}`.

These examples show that a nonzero backtracking defect is already a direct Schwarz/multiplicative-domain effect, before any nontrivial Coxeter face is considered.

### 9.2 Exact six-edge contextual braid loop under dephasing

To reconnect the open-system theory directly to Article I, use the self-adjoint unitaries

`U_1 = sigma_x`,

`U_2 = (sigma_x+sigma_y)/sqrt(2)`,

`U_3 = (sigma_x+sigma_z)/sqrt(2)`.

For an ordering `sigma`, let `W_sigma` be the ordered product. Consider the closed adjacent-swap path

`123 -> 213 -> 231 -> 321 -> 312 -> 132 -> 123`.

For each edge define the exact contextual transport

`T_k := W_{sigma_k} W_{sigma_{k-1}}^*`.

By telescoping,

`T_6...T_1=I`.

Apply the dephasing channel

`Phi_eta([[a,b],[c,d]]) = [[a, eta b],[eta c,d]]`, `0<=eta<=1`.

A direct exact calculation gives

`H_eta := Phi_eta(T_6)...Phi_eta(T_1)`

`= [[ eta^4,  eta^3(1-eta^2)/2 ],`

`   [ -eta^3(1-eta^2)/2, eta^4 ]]`.

Hence

`||H_eta-I||`

`= sqrt( (1-eta^4)^2 + eta^6(1-eta^2)^2/4 )`.

This vanishes at `eta=1` and is nonzero for `0<=eta<1`. For the dephasing semigroup `eta=e^{-gamma t}`,

`||H_t-I|| = sqrt(17) gamma t + O(t^2)`.

Thus an Article-I contextual loop that is exactly flat before reduction acquires a rigorously computable reduced holonomy under dephasing.

The accompanying script compares this exact value with the general defect-bound certificate.

## 10. Relation to standard theory and novelty boundary

The following ingredients are classical and are not novelty claims:

1. Stinespring dilation of completely positive maps — W. F. Stinespring, *Positive Functions on C*-Algebras*, Proc. AMS 6 (1955), DOI `10.1090/S0002-9939-1955-0069403-4`.
2. Schwarz inequalities and the multiplicative domain — M.-D. Choi, *A Schwarz Inequality for Positive Linear Maps on C*-Algebras*, Illinois J. Math. 18 (1974), DOI `10.1215/ijm/1256051007`.
3. bounded generators of quantum dynamical semigroups — G. Lindblad, *On the Generators of Quantum Dynamical Semigroups*, CMP 48 (1976), DOI `10.1007/BF01608499`.
4. finite-dimensional GKSL form — V. Gorini, A. Kossakowski, E. C. G. Sudarshan, J. Math. Phys. 17 (1976), DOI `10.1063/1.522979`.
5. modern quantum-channel multiplicative-domain structure — M. Rahaman, *Multiplicative Properties of Quantum Channels*, arXiv:1701.06205.
6. noncommutative carré-du-champ constructions for quantum Markov semigroups are established literature; our `Gamma_L` uses a sign convention adapted to the multiplicativity defect.

The proposed new synthesis is narrower:

- start from an exactly flat operator-order contextual connection;
- apply a declared UCP/CP context reduction;
- decompose its resulting loop defect exactly into multiplicativity defects;
- resolve multiplicativity loss layer by layer under nested reductions;
- integrate the GKSL Leibniz defect exactly along a uniformly continuous semigroup;
- evaluate the resulting order holonomy on explicit permutation/Coxeter loops.

Priority beyond this precise synthesis requires a dedicated specialist novelty audit before publication.

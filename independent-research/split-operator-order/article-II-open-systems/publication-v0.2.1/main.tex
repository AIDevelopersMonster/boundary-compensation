\begin{abstract}
We study what happens when an exactly flat contextual operator-order transport is reduced edgewise by a unital completely positive (UCP) map. The relevant obstruction is not nonunitarity by itself but failure of multiplicativity. For a UCP map $\Phi$ we use the standard multiplicativity defect
\[
\Delta_\Phi(X,Y)=\Phi(XY)-\Phi(X)\Phi(Y)
\]
and prove an exact decomposition of every reduced flat-loop defect into transported multiplicativity defects. The same defect obeys an exact composition law under nested reductions and has a Stinespring leakage representation. For a uniformly continuous UCP semigroup $\Phi_t=e^{t\mathcal L}$, the multiplicativity defect satisfies an exact Duhamel formula, and for a bounded GKSL generator the corresponding Leibniz defect is
\[
\Gamma_{\mathcal L}(X,Y)=\sum_\alpha [V_\alpha^*,X][Y,V_\alpha].
\]
This yields an exact integral representation of reduced loop holonomy and explicit qubit braid-loop formulas for dephasing, depolarization, and amplitude damping.

We then study the inverse problem. Closed-loop finite-channel data need not identify an arbitrary UCP channel, whereas the full infinitesimal Leibniz defect determines a bounded finite-dimensional generator modulo Hamiltonian derivations. In $M_d(\mathbb C)$ the dissipative quotient has real dimension $(d^2-1)^2$, giving the face-count lower bound $L_d^{\Cox}\ge \lfloor d^2/2\rfloor$. Using block extension, centered scalar-one tangent geometry, binder-compatible transversality, a transverse native-tilt argument, and a determinant-one odd-to-even transfer, we prove the matching upper bound. Thus, in the declared bounded finite-dimensional matrix-valued first-order Coxeter-face measurement model,
\[
\boxed{L_d^{\Cox}=\left\lfloor\frac{d^2}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad d\ge 3.}
\]
This sharp count is not a universal lower bound for arbitrary process tomography or arbitrary Lindbladian-learning protocols. Conditioning, sample complexity, noise robustness, and finite-time channel reconstruction remain separate questions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and claim boundary}
Let $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ be unital $C^*$-algebras and let $\Phi:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ be UCP. Let $T_1,\dots,T_m\in\mathcal A$ be unitary contextual edge transports satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-loop}
T_m\cdots T_1=I.
\end{equation}
Define the reduced loop product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reduced-loop}
H_\Phi:=\Phi(T_m)\cdots\Phi(T_1).
\end{equation}
The term \emph{reduced order holonomy} means only the product defect generated by edgewise reduction of the declared exactly flat operator-order loop. It is not spacetime curvature, not physical gauge curvature, and not the Uhlmann/Jamio\l kowski channel holonomy of smooth channel families.

The paper has two layers: exact reduced-loop/open-system defect calculus, and finite-dimensional first-order identifiability with sharp Coxeter-face design. All sharp tomography statements refer to matrix-valued first-order Coxeter-face coefficients.

\section{Exact multiplicativity-defect calculus}
\begin{definition}[Multiplicativity defect]\label{def:delta}
For $X,Y\in\mathcal A$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
\Delta_\Phi(X,Y):=\Phi(XY)-\Phi(X)\Phi(Y).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
Put $P_k:=T_k\cdots T_1$ and $Q_k:=\Phi(T_k)\cdots\Phi(T_1)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact reduced-loop decomposition]\label{thm:loop-decomp}
For every unital linear map $\Phi$ for which the displayed products are defined,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loop-decomp}
H_\Phi-I
=-\sum_{k=2}^{m}
\Phi(T_m)\cdots\Phi(T_{k+1})
\Delta_\Phi(T_k,P_{k-1}),
\end{equation}
where the leading product is $I$ when $k=m$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $E_k:=Q_k-\Phi(P_k)$. Then $E_1=0$ and
\[
E_k=\Phi(T_k)E_{k-1}-\Delta_\Phi(T_k,P_{k-1}).
\]
Iteration gives \eqref{eq:loop-decomp}. Since $P_m=I$ and $\Phi(I)=I$, $E_m=H_\Phi-I$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[UCP norm certificate]\label{cor:norm}
If $\Phi$ is UCP and all $T_j$ are unitary, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm-cert}
\|H_\Phi-I\|\le \sum_{k=2}^{m}\|\Delta_\Phi(T_k,P_{k-1})\|.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A unital completely positive map is contractive, hence $\|\Phi(T_j)\|\le 1$. Apply the triangle inequality to \eqref{eq:loop-decomp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Multiplicative-domain flatness]\label{cor:md-flat}
If the $C^*$-algebra generated by the transports is contained in $\MD(\Phi)$, then $H_\Phi=I$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All defects in \eqref{eq:loop-decomp} vanish on the multiplicative domain.
\end{proof}

\section{Nested reductions}
\begin{theorem}[Composition law]\label{thm:composition}
For composable unital linear maps $\Phi$ and $\Psi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composition}
\Delta_{\Psi\circ\Phi}(X,Y)
=\Psi\bigl(\Delta_\Phi(X,Y)\bigr)
+\Delta_\Psi\bigl(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Insert and subtract $\Psi(\Phi(X)\Phi(Y))$ in the definition of $\Delta_{\Psi\circ\Phi}$.
\end{proof}

For a chain $\mathcal A_0\xrightarrow{\Phi_1}\cdots\xrightarrow{\Phi_r}\mathcal A_r$ let $\Phi_{[j:1]}:=\Phi_j\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_1$.

\begin{corollary}[Layer-resolved multiplicativity loss]\label{cor:layers}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:layers}
\Delta_{\Phi_{[r:1]}}(X,Y)
=\sum_{j=1}^{r}
(\Phi_r\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{j+1})
\left[
\Delta_{\Phi_j}\bigl(\Phi_{[j-1:1]}(X),\Phi_{[j-1:1]}(Y)\bigr)
\right].
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Iterate \cref{thm:composition}.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{eq:layers} does not imply a universal scalar monotonicity law for $\|\Delta\|$ or $\|H-I\|$: a downstream reduction both transports existing defects and may create new ones.

\section{Backtracking and Stinespring leakage}
For a unitary $U$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schwarz-defects}
D_R^\Phi(U):=I-\Phi(U)^*\Phi(U),\qquad
D_L^\Phi(U):=I-\Phi(U)\Phi(U)^*.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Two Schwarz defects]\label{thm:schwarz}
For UCP $\Phi$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schwarz-delta}
D_R^\Phi(U)=\Delta_\Phi(U^*,U)\ge0,\qquad
D_L^\Phi(U)=\Delta_\Phi(U,U^*)\ge0.
\end{equation}
Moreover $U\in\MD(\Phi)$ if and only if both defects vanish.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Unitality gives $\Phi(U^*U)=\Phi(UU^*)=I$. The Schwarz inequalities yield \eqref{eq:schwarz-delta}; simultaneous equality is the standard multiplicative-domain criterion for a unitary.
\end{proof}

Let $\Phi(X)=V^*\pi(X)V$ be a Stinespring representation with $V$ an isometry and $P:=VV^*$.

\begin{proposition}[Stinespring defect formula]\label{prop:stinespring}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stinespring}
\Delta_\Phi(X,Y)=V^*\pi(X)(I-P)\pi(Y)V.
\end{equation}
For unitary $U$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stinespring-square}
D_R^\Phi(U)=\bigl((I-P)\pi(U)V\bigr)^*\bigl((I-P)\pi(U)V\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Insert $I=P+(I-P)$ between $\pi(X)$ and $\pi(Y)$. The $P$ term is $\Phi(X)\Phi(Y)$. Equation \eqref{eq:stinespring-square} is the case $(X,Y)=(U^*,U)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Uniformly continuous UCP semigroups}
Let $\Phi_t=e^{t\mathcal L}$ be a uniformly continuous unital CP semigroup with bounded generator $\mathcal L$. For fixed $X,Y$ define
\[
\Delta_t(X,Y):=\Phi_t(XY)-\Phi_t(X)\Phi_t(Y),
\]
and define the Leibniz defect
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
\Gamma_{\mathcal L}(X,Y):=\mathcal L(XY)-\mathcal L(X)Y-X\mathcal L(Y).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact defect evolution]\label{thm:defect-evolution}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect-evolution}
\frac{d}{dt}\Delta_t(X,Y)
=\mathcal L\bigl(\Delta_t(X,Y)\bigr)
+\Gamma_{\mathcal L}\bigl(\Phi_t(X),\Phi_t(Y)\bigr),
\qquad \Delta_0=0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate $\Phi_t(XY)-\Phi_t(X)\Phi_t(Y)$ and insert $\Phi_t(XY)=\Delta_t+\Phi_t(X)\Phi_t(Y)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Duhamel formula]\label{cor:duhamel}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:duhamel}
\Delta_t(X,Y)
=\int_0^t \Phi_{t-s}\!\left(
\Gamma_{\mathcal L}(\Phi_s(X),\Phi_s(Y))
\right)ds.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply variation of constants to \eqref{eq:defect-evolution}.
\end{proof}

Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gksl}
\mathcal L(X)=i[H,X]+\sum_\alpha\left(V_\alpha^*XV_\alpha-
\frac12\{V_\alpha^*V_\alpha,X\}\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[GKSL Leibniz defect]\label{thm:gksl-gamma}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gksl-gamma}
\Gamma_{\mathcal L}(X,Y)=\sum_\alpha[V_\alpha^*,X][Y,V_\alpha].
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-gamma}
\Gamma_{\mathcal L}(A^*,A)=\sum_\alpha[A,V_\alpha]^*[A,V_\alpha]\ge0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Hamiltonian commutator is a derivation and cancels in \eqref{eq:gamma}. Expanding one dissipative term gives
$V^*XYV-V^*XVY-XV^*YV+XV^*VY=[V^*,X][Y,V]$. Sum over $\alpha$.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact Lindblad loop holonomy}
\begin{theorem}[Exact integral representation]\label{thm:lindblad-loop}
For the flat loop \eqref{eq:flat-loop},
\begin{align}\label{eq:lindblad-loop}
H_t-I
&=-\sum_{k=2}^{m}\Phi_t(T_m)\cdots\Phi_t(T_{k+1})\\
&\quad\times\int_0^t
\Phi_{t-s}\!\left(
\Gamma_{\mathcal L}(\Phi_s(T_k),\Phi_s(P_{k-1}))
\right)ds.
\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:duhamel} into \eqref{eq:loop-decomp} with $\Phi=\Phi_t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[First-order coefficient]\label{cor:first-order}
As $t\to0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-order}
H_t-I=-t\sum_{k=2}^{m}T_m\cdots T_{k+1}
\Gamma_{\mathcal L}(T_k,P_{k-1})+O(t^2)
\end{equation}
in operator norm.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Expand every bounded semigroup factor and the Duhamel integrand at $t=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact qubit braid-loop examples}
Use
\[
U_1=\sigma_x,\qquad
U_2=\frac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{\sqrt2},\qquad
U_3=\frac{\sigma_x+\sigma_z}{\sqrt2}
\]
and the adjacent-swap loop
\[
123\to213\to231\to321\to312\to132\to123.
\]
The exact contextual edge transports $T_1,\dots,T_6$ telescope to $I$.

\subsection{Dephasing}
For
\[
\Phi_\eta\!\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}a&\eta b\\ \eta c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0\le\eta\le1,
\]
direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dephasing-H}
H_\eta=
\begin{pmatrix}
\eta^4&\frac12\eta^3(1-\eta^2)\\
-\frac12\eta^3(1-\eta^2)&\eta^4
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dephasing-norm}
\|H_\eta-I\|
=\sqrt{(1-\eta^4)^2+\frac14\eta^6(1-\eta^2)^2}.
\end{equation}
For $\eta=e^{-\gamma t}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dephasing-linear}
\|H_t-I\|=\sqrt{17}\,\gamma t+O(t^2).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Depolarization}
For $\Phi_\eta(A)=\eta A+(1-\eta)\Tr(A)I/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depolarizing-H}
H_\eta^{\mathrm{dep}}=
\begin{pmatrix}a_\eta&b_\eta\\-\overline{b_\eta}&a_\eta\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where
\[
a_\eta=\frac12\eta^5(\eta+1),\qquad
b_\eta=\frac14\eta^4(1-\eta)\bigl(2\eta+i(1+\eta)\bigr).
\]
For $\eta=e^{-\gamma t}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depolarizing-linear}
\|H_t^{\mathrm{dep}}-I\|=\frac{\sqrt{123}}2\,\gamma t+O(t^2).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Amplitude damping}
For the Heisenberg-picture amplitude-damping channel with survival parameter $q=e^{-\gamma t}$, the exact loop matrix is generally non-normal. Its first-order norm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ad-linear}
\|H_t^{\mathrm{AD}}-I\|
=\frac12\sqrt{57+2\sqrt{314}}\,\gamma t+O(t^2).
\end{equation}
These coefficients are loop-resolved fingerprints relative to the declared loop, not universal channel invariants.

\section{Finite-time no-go and infinitesimal identifiability}
Closed-loop finite-channel data can fail to identify an arbitrary UCP channel. In the qubit depolarizing family there is a transport family for which every contextual edge is traceless and every closed adjacent-transposition loop has even length. Therefore $\Phi_r$ and $\Phi_{-r}$, $0<r\le1/3$, have identical reduced holonomy on every closed loop although the channels are distinct.

The infinitesimal problem has a different target.

\begin{proposition}[Full Leibniz defect identifies modulo derivations]\label{prop:gamma-ident}
Let $\mathcal L_1,\mathcal L_2:\Md\to\Md$ be bounded, unital and $*$-preserving. Then
\[
\Gamma_{\mathcal L_1}=\Gamma_{\mathcal L_2}
\]
on all pairs if and only if $\mathcal L_1-\mathcal L_2$ is a derivation. Hence there is a self-adjoint $H_0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mod-derivation}
\mathcal L_1-\mathcal L_2=i[H_0,\cdot].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equality of the two $\Gamma$ forms is exactly the Leibniz rule for $D:=\mathcal L_1-\mathcal L_2$. Every derivation of a full matrix algebra is inner; $*$-preservation gives Hamiltonian form up to a scalar implementer.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-dimensional quotient and rank criterion}
Let $q=d^2-1$. The real vector space of complex-linear, unital, $*$-preserving maps $\Md\to\Md$ has dimension $d^2(d^2-1)$. Hamiltonian derivations have real dimension $d^2-1$. Therefore the quotient dimension is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-dim}
N_d=(d^2-1)^2.
\end{equation}

For a finite Coxeter-loop design $\mathscr D$, all selected real and imaginary matrix entries of the first-order coefficients define a real-linear measurement map $M_{\mathscr D}$ on this quotient.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-dimensional rank criterion]\label{thm:rank-criterion}
The design identifies every bounded generator modulo Hamiltonian derivations if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-rank}
\rank M_{\mathscr D}=(d^2-1)^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The selected coefficients depend real-linearly on the quotient class by \cref{prop:gamma-ident}. Universal identifiability is injectivity of $M_{\mathscr D}$, which is equivalent to full column rank.
\end{proof}

A general matrix-valued face lies in $M_d(\mathbb C)$ and provides at most $2d^2$ real scalar coordinates. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-bound-ceil}
L_d^{\Cox}\ge
\left\lceil\frac{(d^2-1)^2}{2d^2}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
For every integer $d\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-bound}
\left\lceil\frac{(d^2-1)^2}{2d^2}\right\rceil
=\left\lfloor\frac{d^2}{2}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Indeed $(d^2-1)^2/(2d^2)=d^2/2-1+1/(2d^2)$.

Exact finite-field certificates attain the lower bound in $d=3,4,5$ with ranks $64,225,576$ from $4,8,12$ faces, respectively. These are independent checks and are not used as extrapolative evidence for the all-dimensional theorem.

\section{Restriction ceiling and extension-ready designs}
Embed $M_d(\mathbb C)$ into $M_{d+1}(\mathbb C)$ by $A\mapsto\diag(A,1)$ and set $B=M_d(\mathbb C)\oplus\mathbb C$.

\begin{theorem}[Restriction quotient dimension]\label{thm:restriction}
The real dimension of normalized complex-linear $*$-preserving maps $B\to M_{d+1}(\mathbb C)$ modulo restricted Hamiltonian derivations equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rres}
R_{\mathrm{res}}(d)=(d^2-1)(d+1)^2+2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Hermitian part of $B$ has real dimension $d^2+1$, so normalized $*$-preserving maps have dimension $d^2(d+1)^2$. Restricted inner derivations have dimension $(d+1)^2-2$ because the Hermitian commutant of $B$ is two-dimensional. Subtraction gives \eqref{eq:Rres}.
\end{proof}

For a lower-bound-sized design $L_d=\lfloor d^2/2\rfloor$, row capacity and \eqref{eq:Rres} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedded-ceiling}
\rank M_d^{\uparrow}\le
(d^2-1)(d+1)^2+2\mathbf 1_{d\ \mathrm{even}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Extension-ready minimal design]\label{def:er}
A dimension-$d$ Coxeter design is extension-ready minimal if it has exactly $\lfloor d^2/2\rfloor$ faces, native rank $(d^2-1)^2$, and embedded rank equal to the ceiling \eqref{eq:embedded-ceiling}.
\end{definition}
Native full rank alone does not imply extension-readiness.

\section{Sharp theorem: proof architecture}
The sharp construction is proved in the appendices. The core ingredients are stated here so the dependency of the final theorem is explicit.

For odd $n\ge3$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lodd}
L=\frac{n^2-1}{2}.
\end{equation}
In the scalar-one centered coordinate $G(A)=F(A-I)$, tangent transports $e^{\varepsilon X},e^{\varepsilon Y}$ with $X,Y\in\mathfrak{sl}_n$ have first nonzero coefficient of order $\varepsilon^2$. The four embedded sectors are
\begin{align}
\delta_{\mathrm{reg}}F(X,Y)&=F(XY)-F(X)Y-XF(Y),\label{eq:centered-reg}\\
\delta_LF(X,Y)&=F(XY)-XF(Y),\label{eq:centered-left}\\
\delta_RF(X,Y)&=F(XY)-F(X)Y,\label{eq:centered-right}\\
\delta_0F(X,Y)&=F(XY).\label{eq:centered-scalar}
\end{align}

Fix a traceless simple-spectrum diagonal anchor $H$. The complete regular one-anchor kernel consists of an off-diagonal inner derivation plus an arbitrary off-diagonal Schur multiplier. A cyclic-shift binder reduces the Schur coefficients to the path-additive form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-additive}
c_{rs}=a_s+a_{s+1}+\cdots+a_{r-1},
\end{equation}
leaving, modulo diagonal derivations, one cycle-holonomy coordinate $q:=\sum_i a_i$.

Exactly $L$ paired $H$-anchored faces can realize the complete anchor rowspace. Their left dependency space has dimension $n(n-2)$. Perturbing the last anchor by $H\mapsto H+tS$ produces a compressed dependency operator $D_n$. The cycle-factor argument reduces $D_n$ to an odd-cycle pivot and one scalar coefficient $\theta_e$ per non-cycle directed edge. Binder-compatible transversality proves all these coefficients can be nonzero simultaneously on the reverse-cycle-zero subspace actually required by \eqref{eq:path-additive}.

The one-parameter $H+tS$ deformation leaves the cycle-holonomy line in the native hyperplane $F(I)=0$ at all orders. A transverse reverse-cycle perturbation $Y_*\mapsto Y_*+sE_{r-1,r}$ creates diagonal spikes proportional to $-tsq$. A finite reconstruction argument then proves that for a suitable $r$ the native projection coefficient is nonzero.

These results yield the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}[All-odd extension-ready existence]\label{thm:all-odd-er}
For every odd $n\ge3$, there exists an extension-ready minimal Coxeter design with exactly $(n^2-1)/2$ faces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The detailed centered tangent construction, compressed dependency nonvanishing, binder-subspace transversality repair, native-tilt calculation, and tangent-to-finite lifting are proved in \cref{app:odd-proof}. Their finite intersection gives simultaneous scalar, left, right, regular embedded maxima and native full rank. Because the tangent transports are exponentials of traceless matrices, the selected nonzero minors lift analytically to determinant-one finite transports and hence to genuine finite Coxeter-square designs.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Odd-to-even extension-ready transfer]\label{thm:odd-even}
For every odd $d\ge3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:er-transfer}
\mathrm{ER}_d\Longrightarrow \mathrm{ER}_{d+1},
\end{equation}
using exactly $d+1$ new faces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The determinant-one centered scalar-one transfer is proved in \cref{app:transfer-proof}. The construction uses one carrier face to repair the five missing old restriction coordinates and $d$ local/binding faces to resolve the relative quotient modulo exactly the three-dimensional Hamiltonian gauge. All complex witnesses lie in $\SL_{d+1}(\mathbb C)$ before any rank calculation. Zariski density of $\SU(d+1)$ and the engineered contextual-square realization return the construction to genuine unitary Coxeter faces.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp all-dimensional Coxeter face count]\label{thm:sharp}
For every integer $d\ge3$, the minimum number of matrix-valued first-order adjacent-transposition Coxeter faces required for universal identification of a bounded unital $*$-preserving generator on $M_d(\mathbb C)$ modulo Hamiltonian derivations is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
\boxed{L_d^{\Cox}=\left\lfloor\frac{d^2}{2}\right\rfloor.}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound is \eqref{eq:lower-bound}. If $d$ is odd, \cref{thm:all-odd-er} attains the bound. If $d$ is even, apply \cref{thm:odd-even} to the preceding odd dimension $d-1$. The resulting face count is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:even-count-corrected}
\frac{(d-1)^2-1}{2}+d=\frac{d^2}{2}.
\end{equation}
Thus the lower bound is attained in every dimension $d\ge3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[$d=2$ boundary]\label{rem:d2}
No two-face design is extension-ready under the scalar-one embedding $M_2\hookrightarrow M_3$. The obstruction is specific to extension-readiness: it does not by itself rule out native two-face tomography in $d=2$. The sharp theorem is intentionally stated only for $d\ge3$.
\end{remark}

\section{Earlier upper bounds and reproducibility}
Earlier constructions proved $L_d^{\Cox}\le3d^2-1$ and later $L_d^{\Cox}\le2d^2$. They remain valid constructive designs and are useful when explicit transport families are more important than minimal face count. They are no longer the best count theorem.

The repository contains deterministic scripts for exact qubit and qutrit checks, exact $d=4$ and $d=5$ finite-field certificates, and the qubit braid-loop channel calculations. The $d=5$ builder reconstructs a $600\times576$ matrix over $\mathbb F_{1000033}$ and obtains rank $576$. The all-dimensional proof does not extrapolate from these computations.

\section{Relation to existing literature and claim firewall}
Stinespring dilation, Choi's Schwarz inequality and multiplicative domain, the GKSL/Lindblad generator representation, noncommutative carre-du-champ structures, and general Lindbladian/Liouvillian tomography are standard independent infrastructure. The channel holonomy of Kult, \AA berg and Sj\"oqvist is based on Jamio\l kowski/Uhlmann-type parallel transport for smooth channel families and is mathematically different from the reduced product defect studied here.

The contribution claimed here is narrower: coupling exact operator-order flatness to UCP multiplicativity loss, deriving the resulting exact loop and semigroup identities, and solving the declared finite-dimensional first-order Coxeter-face design problem.

The paper does not prove finite-time UCP-channel identifiability from closed loops, conditioning or sample-complexity bounds, robustness to preparation/measurement noise, universal monotonicity under further CP reductions, equality with entropy-production/recoverability/channel-capacity quantities, infinite-dimensional unbounded-generator analogues, non-Markovian process-tensor analogues, or physical spacetime/gauge curvature.

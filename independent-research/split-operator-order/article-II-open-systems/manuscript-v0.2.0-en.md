## Abstract

We study what happens when an exactly flat contextual operator-order transport is reduced edgewise by a unital completely positive (UCP) map. The relevant obstruction is not nonunitarity by itself, but failure of multiplicativity. For a UCP map `Phi` we use the standard multiplicativity defect

`Delta_Phi(X,Y)=Phi(XY)-Phi(X)Phi(Y)`

and prove an exact decomposition of every reduced flat-loop defect into transported multiplicativity defects. The same defect obeys an exact composition law under nested reductions and has a Stinespring leakage representation. For a uniformly continuous UCP semigroup `Phi_t=exp(tL)`, the multiplicativity defect satisfies an exact Duhamel formula, and for a bounded GKSL generator the corresponding Leibniz defect is

`Gamma_L(X,Y)=sum_alpha [V_alpha^*,X][Y,V_alpha]`.

This yields an exact integral representation of reduced loop holonomy and explicit qubit braid-loop formulas for dephasing, depolarization, and amplitude damping.

We then study the inverse problem. Closed-loop finite-channel data need not identify an arbitrary UCP channel, but the full infinitesimal Leibniz defect determines a bounded finite-dimensional generator modulo Hamiltonian derivations. In `M_d(C)` the dissipative quotient has real dimension `(d^2-1)^2`, giving the face-count lower bound

`L_d^Cox >= floor(d^2/2)`.

Using a block-extension programme, centered scalar-one tangent geometry, binder-compatible transversality, a transverse native-tilt argument, and a repaired odd-to-even determinant-one transfer, we prove the matching upper bound. Thus in the declared bounded finite-dimensional first-order Coxeter-face measurement model,

`L_d^Cox=floor(d^2/2)`

for every `d>=3`.

This sharp count is not a universal lower bound for arbitrary quantum process tomography or Lindbladian learning. Conditioning, sampling complexity, noise robustness, and finite-time channel reconstruction are separate questions.

## 1. Problem, scope, and claim boundary

Let `A` and `B` be unital C*-algebras and let

`Phi:A->B`

be UCP. Let

`T_1,...,T_m in A`

be unitary contextual edge transports satisfying

`T_m...T_1=I`.

The unreduced contextual transport is therefore exactly flat. Define

`H_Phi:=Phi(T_m)...Phi(T_1)`.

In general `H_Phi!=I` because `Phi` need not preserve multiplication.

The term **reduced order holonomy** in this paper means only the product defect generated by edgewise reduction of the declared exactly flat operator-order loop. It is not spacetime curvature, not a physical gauge curvature, and not the channel holonomy defined from Uhlmann/Jamiołkowski parallel transport for smooth channel families.

The paper has two mathematical layers:

1. exact reduced-loop and open-system defect calculus;
2. finite-dimensional first-order identifiability and sharp Coxeter-face design.

All sharp tomography statements are finite-dimensional and refer to matrix-valued first-order Coxeter-face coefficients.

## 2. Exact multiplicativity-defect calculus

### Definition 2.1 — multiplicativity defect

For `X,Y in A`, define

`Delta_Phi(X,Y):=Phi(XY)-Phi(X)Phi(Y)`.

Put

`P_k:=T_k...T_1`,

`Q_k:=Phi(T_k)...Phi(T_1)`.

### Theorem 2.2 — exact reduced-loop decomposition

For every unital linear map `Phi` for which the products are defined,

`H_Phi-I`

`= - sum_(k=2)^m Phi(T_m)...Phi(T_(k+1)) Delta_Phi(T_k,P_(k-1))`,

with the leading product interpreted as `I` when `k=m`.

#### Proof

Set

`E_k:=Q_k-Phi(P_k)`.

Then `E_1=0` and

`E_k=Phi(T_k)E_(k-1)-Delta_Phi(T_k,P_(k-1))`.

Iteration gives the displayed sum. Since `P_m=I` and `Phi(I)=I`, one has `E_m=H_Phi-I`. QED.

### Corollary 2.3 — UCP norm certificate

If `Phi` is UCP and all `T_k` are unitary,

`||H_Phi-I|| <= sum_(k=2)^m ||Delta_Phi(T_k,P_(k-1))||`.

### Corollary 2.4 — multiplicative-domain flatness

If the C*-algebra generated by the transports is contained in the multiplicative domain `MD(Phi)`, then

`H_Phi=I`.

The converse need not hold for one particular loop because nonzero defects may cancel.

## 3. Nested reductions

Let

`A_0 --Phi_1--> A_1 --Phi_2--> ... --Phi_r--> A_r`

be composable unital linear maps.

### Theorem 3.1 — composition law

For composable `Phi,Psi`,

`Delta_(Psi o Phi)(X,Y)`

`=Psi(Delta_Phi(X,Y))+Delta_Psi(Phi(X),Phi(Y))`.

#### Proof

Insert and subtract `Psi(Phi(X)Phi(Y))`. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — layer-resolved multiplicativity loss

Let

`Phi_[j:1]:=Phi_j o ... o Phi_1`.

Then

`Delta_(Phi_[r:1])(X,Y)`

`=sum_(j=1)^r Phi_r o ... o Phi_(j+1)(`

` Delta_(Phi_j)(Phi_[j-1:1](X),Phi_[j-1:1](Y)) )`.

This identity does not imply a universal scalar monotonicity theorem for `||Delta||` or `||H-I||`: each reduction both transports older defects and may create new ones.

## 4. Backtracking and Stinespring leakage

For a unitary `U`, define

`D_R^Phi(U):=I-Phi(U)^*Phi(U)`,

`D_L^Phi(U):=I-Phi(U)Phi(U)^*`.

### Theorem 4.1 — two Schwarz defects

For UCP `Phi`,

`D_R^Phi(U)=Delta_Phi(U^*,U)>=0`,

`D_L^Phi(U)=Delta_Phi(U,U^*)>=0`.

Consequently,

`U in MD(Phi)`

if and only if both defects vanish.

Now let

`Phi(X)=V^* pi(X)V`

be a Stinespring representation and put `P=VV^*`.

### Proposition 4.2 — Stinespring defect formula

`Delta_Phi(X,Y)=V^* pi(X)(I-P)pi(Y)V`.

For unitary `U`,

`D_R^Phi(U)=((I-P)pi(U)V)^*((I-P)pi(U)V)`.

Thus the right backtracking defect is a squared dilation leakage operator.

## 5. Uniformly continuous UCP semigroups

Let

`Phi_t=exp(tL)`

be a uniformly continuous unital CP semigroup, with bounded generator `L`.

Define

`Gamma_L(X,Y):=L(XY)-L(X)Y-XL(Y)`.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact defect evolution

For fixed `X,Y`,

`d/dt Delta_t(X,Y)`

`=L(Delta_t(X,Y))+Gamma_L(Phi_t(X),Phi_t(Y))`,

with `Delta_0=0`.

### Corollary 5.2 — Duhamel formula

`Delta_t(X,Y)`

`= integral_0^t Phi_(t-s)(Gamma_L(Phi_s(X),Phi_s(Y))) ds`.

For a bounded Heisenberg-picture GKSL generator

`L(X)=i[H,X] + sum_alpha (V_alpha^* X V_alpha - 1/2{V_alpha^*V_alpha,X})`,

one obtains:

### Theorem 5.3 — GKSL Leibniz defect

`Gamma_L(X,Y)=sum_alpha [V_alpha^*,X][Y,V_alpha]`.

In particular,

`Gamma_L(A^*,A)=sum_alpha [A,V_alpha]^*[A,V_alpha]>=0`.

## 6. Exact Lindblad loop holonomy

For the original flat loop define

`H_t:=Phi_t(T_m)...Phi_t(T_1)`.

### Theorem 6.1 — exact integral representation

`H_t-I`

`= - sum_(k=2)^m Phi_t(T_m)...Phi_t(T_(k+1))`

`  integral_0^t Phi_(t-s)(Gamma_L(Phi_s(T_k),Phi_s(P_(k-1)))) ds`.

The first-order coefficient is therefore

`H_t-I`

`= -t sum_(k=2)^m T_m...T_(k+1) Gamma_L(T_k,P_(k-1)) + O(t^2)`.

This first-order expression is a linearization of an exact integral theorem.

## 7. Exact braid-loop channel examples

Use the Article-I qubit transport triple

`U_1=sigma_x`,

`U_2=(sigma_x+sigma_y)/sqrt(2)`,

`U_3=(sigma_x+sigma_z)/sqrt(2)`

and the adjacent-swap loop

`123 -> 213 -> 231 -> 321 -> 312 -> 132 -> 123`.

Let its exact edge transports be `T_1,...,T_6`.

### 7.1 Dephasing

For

`Phi_eta([[a,b],[c,d]])=[[a,eta b],[eta c,d]]`,

`0<=eta<=1`,

one obtains

`H_eta=[[eta^4, eta^3(1-eta^2)/2],[-eta^3(1-eta^2)/2,eta^4]]`.

Hence

`||H_eta-I||`

`=sqrt((1-eta^4)^2+eta^6(1-eta^2)^2/4)`.

For `eta=e^(-gamma t)`,

`||H_t-I||=sqrt(17) gamma t+O(t^2)`.

### 7.2 Depolarizing channel

For

`Phi_eta(A)=eta A+(1-eta)tr(A)I/2`,

the exact braid-loop matrix is

`H_eta^dep=[[a_eta,b_eta],[-conj(b_eta),a_eta]]`,

with

`a_eta=eta^5(eta+1)/2`,

`b_eta=eta^4(1-eta)[2eta+i(1+eta)]/4`.

For `eta=e^(-gamma t)`,

`||H_t^dep-I||=(sqrt(123)/2)gamma t+O(t^2)`.

### 7.3 Amplitude damping

For the Heisenberg-picture amplitude-damping channel with survival parameter `q`, the exact braid-loop matrix is generally non-normal. Its first-order norm coefficient under `q=e^(-gamma t)` is

`||H_t^AD-I||`

`= (1/2)sqrt(57+2sqrt(314)) gamma t+O(t^2)`.

These are loop-resolved fingerprints relative to the declared contextual loop. They are not universal channel invariants or decoherence monotones.

## 8. Finite-channel no-go and infinitesimal identifiability

Closed-loop holonomy data can fail to determine an arbitrary finite-time UCP channel.

A concrete qubit depolarizing construction gives two distinct UCP channels `Phi_r` and `Phi_(-r)`, `0<r<=1/3`, with identical reduced holonomy on every closed adjacent-transposition loop of a suitable transport family. Thus complete closed-loop data can be many-to-one at finite channel time.

The infinitesimal problem is different.

### Proposition 8.1 — full `Gamma_L` determines the generator modulo derivations

If bounded unital *-preserving maps `L_1,L_2:M_d(C)->M_d(C)` satisfy

`Gamma_(L_1)=Gamma_(L_2)`

on all pairs, then

`L_1-L_2`

is a derivation. Since derivations of `M_d(C)` are inner, for *-preserving maps

`L_1-L_2=i[H_0,.]`

for some self-adjoint `H_0`.

Thus the natural infinitesimal inverse target is the generator modulo Hamiltonian motion.

## 9. Finite-dimensional quotient and rank criterion

Let `q=d^2-1`.

The real vector space of complex-linear, unital, *-preserving maps `M_d(C)->M_d(C)` has dimension

`d^2(d^2-1)`.

Hamiltonian derivations have real dimension `d^2-1`. Therefore the quotient dimension is

`N_d=(d^2-1)^2`.

For a finite Coxeter-loop design `D`, the first-order matrix coefficients define a real-linear measurement operator

`M_D : G_d/Der(M_d) -> R^N`.

### Theorem 9.1 — finite-dimensional identifiability criterion

The design identifies every bounded dissipative generator modulo Hamiltonian derivations if and only if

`rank M_D=(d^2-1)^2`.

A general matrix-valued face contributes at most `2d^2` real scalar coordinates. Hence any face-only universally identifying design satisfies

`L_d^Cox >= ceil((d^2-1)^2/(2d^2))`.

For every integer `d>=2`, this simplifies to

`L_d^Cox >= floor(d^2/2)`.

## 10. Exact low-dimensional certificates

The lower bound is attained exactly in dimensions `d=3,4,5` by explicit finite-field-certified designs.

- `d=3`: four faces, rank `64`;
- `d=4`: eight faces, rank `225`;
- `d=5`: twelve faces, rank `576`.

The `d=5` script rebuilds a `600 x 576` matrix over the finite field `F_1000033` and returns full rank `576`. The certificate is constructive and does not use a stored rank label.

These finite-dimensional certificates are independent checks of the general theorem below; the all-dimensional proof does not rely on extrapolating numerical rank from `d=3,4,5`.

## 11. Extension-ready designs and the structural ceiling

Embed

`M_d(C) -> M_(d+1)(C)`,

`A -> diag(A,1)`.

Let

`B=M_d(C) direct-sum C`.

Every embedded old-face measurement factors through the restriction of the generator to `B`.

### Theorem 11.1 — restriction quotient dimension

The normalized `*`-preserving restriction quotient has real dimension

`R_res(d)=(d^2-1)(d+1)^2+2`.

Consequently, for a minimal `d`-dimensional design with

`L_d=floor(d^2/2)`, 

the embedded rank satisfies

`rank M_d^up`

`<= (d^2-1)(d+1)^2 + 2*1_(d even)`.

### Definition 11.2 — extension-ready minimal design

A dimension-`d` design is **extension-ready minimal** if:

1. it contains exactly `floor(d^2/2)` faces;
2. it has native rank `(d^2-1)^2`;
3. after scalar-one embedding it attains the structural embedded ceiling.

Native full rank alone does not imply extension-readiness.

## 12. Odd-dimensional centered tangent framework

Let `n>=3` be odd and

`L=(n^2-1)/2`.

For scalar-one block embedding use centered coordinates

`G(A)=F(A-I)`.

For tangent transports

`A_eps=exp(eps X)`, `B_eps=exp(eps Y)`,

with `X,Y in sl_n(C)`, the first nonzero coefficient is order `eps^2`.

The four embedded tangent sectors are:

- regular: `F(XY)-F(X)Y-XF(Y)`;
- left: `F(XY)-X F(Y)`;
- right: `F(XY)-F(X)Y`;
- scalar: `F(XY)`.

The scalar, left, and right sector maxima are each attained on nonempty Zariski-open loci.

For the regular sector, fix a simple-spectrum traceless diagonal anchor `H`.

### Theorem 12.1 — complete one-anchor kernel

The complete kernel of

`delta_F(H,Y)=delta_F(Y,H)=0`

for all traceless `Y` is

`F=ad_(K_off)+S_c`,

where `K_off` is arbitrary off-diagonal and `S_c` is an arbitrary off-diagonal Schur multiplier vanishing on the diagonal algebra.

Its dimension is `2n(n-1)`.

A cyclic-shift binder reduces the Schur part to the `n`-parameter path-additive family

`c_(r,s)=a_s+...+a_(r-1)`.

Modulo diagonal derivations, the only extra direction is the cycle-holonomy coordinate

`q=sum_i a_i`.

## 13. Finite compression and binder-compatible transversality

Exactly

`L=(n^2-1)/2`

paired `H`-anchored faces can realize the complete `H`-anchor rowspace. Their left row-dependency space has dimension

`n(n-2)`.

Perturbing one anchor by

`H -> H+tS`

produces a compressed dependency operator `D_n`. A cycle-factor reduction shows that, after an invertible odd-cycle pivot, `D_n` is triangular in the non-cycle path-defect coordinates, with diagonal coefficients `theta_e`.

The required conditions must hold not merely in the ambient sample space but in the reverse-cycle-zero binder subspace used by the path-additivity construction.

### Theorem 13.1 — binder-compatible good locus

For every odd `n>=3`, there exists a finite complete compression whose last sample has all reverse-cycle entries zero and for which simultaneously:

1. the complete `H`-anchor rank is attained;
2. the odd-cycle pivot is invertible;
3. all non-cycle coefficients `theta_e` are nonzero;
4. the binder is generic;
5. a specified diagonal-block dual identity coefficient is nonzero.

Therefore `D_n` is invertible on the actual parameter space required by the native-tilt proof.

## 14. Native tilt and all-odd extension-readiness

The one-parameter deformation `H->H+tS` does **not** tilt the remaining non-derivation line: the path-additive holonomy multiplier `h_q` remains an exact kernel vector for every `t` and satisfies `h_q(I)=0`.

A transverse perturbation is required. Let

`Z_r=E_(r-1,r)`

be a forbidden reverse-cycle entry and perturb

`Y_* -> Y_*+sZ_r`.

Then

`delta_(h_q)(S,Z_r)=-q E_(r,r)`,

`delta_(h_q)(Z_r,S)=-q E_(r-1,r-1)`.

Thus at fixed nonzero `t`, the holonomy direction is detected linearly in `s`.

Let `M_t` be the embedded-full-rank regular measurement map at `s=0`, and let

`E(F)=F(I)=R_t M_t(F)`.

For the explicit reverse-cycle spike `d_r`, define

`kappa_(n,r)(t):=R_t A_(t,r)h_q`.

A diagonal-block dual-basis construction gives a spike `d_r` with a preimage `F_r^(0)` satisfying

`F_r^(0)(I)!=0`.

A singular Schur-complement argument controlled by invertibility of `D_n` yields

`R_t d_r -> F_r^(0)(I)`

as `t->0`.

Therefore, for sufficiently small nonzero `t`,

`kappa_(n,r)(t)!=0`.

### Theorem 14.1 — all-odd extension-ready existence

For every odd `n>=3`, there exists an extension-ready minimal Coxeter design with exactly

`(n^2-1)/2`

faces.

The tangent witness lifts analytically to genuine determinant-one finite transports because all tangent generators are traceless.

## 15. Odd-to-even determinant-one transfer

Let `d>=3` be odd, `n=d+1`, and assume an extension-ready minimal design in dimension `d`.

The odd-to-even transfer is performed directly in the exact scalar-one centered convention

`D(diag(X,a))=F(X-aI)`.

No post hoc scalar determinant normalization is used.

A carrier face is parameterized directly in `SL_n(C)` by

`A=diag(C,(det C)^(-1))`,

`B=diag(D,(det D)^(-1))`.

It repairs the five missing old restriction coordinates and reduces the new diagonal relative variable to the two off-diagonal tail gauge directions.

The remaining `d` local faces admit a two-tail reduction with determinant

`- (lambda_j-1)(lambda_j-lambda_r lambda_s)/(lambda_j lambda_r)`.

A determinant-one first perturbation binds the far-tail slopes to the two carrier gauge coordinates. A determinant-one cross-plane perturbation then gives the graph relations

`alpha_j+gamma_k=0`,

`alpha_k+gamma_j=0`.

Choosing a connected non-bipartite graph leaves exactly the three-dimensional relative Hamiltonian gauge.

### Theorem 15.1 — odd-to-even extension-ready transfer

For every odd `d>=3`,

`ER_d -> ER_(d+1)`

using exactly `d+1` new faces.

All complex witnesses lie in `SL_(d+1)(C)` before rank calculation. Since `SU(d+1)` is Zariski dense in `SL_(d+1)(C)`, the nonzero maximal minor occurs at a unitary determinant-one point. The engineered-square realization theorem converts each ordered transport pair into a genuine adjacent-transposition Coxeter square.

## 16. Sharp Coxeter face count

We now combine the lower bound with the two parity constructions.

### Theorem 16.1 — sharp all-dimensional Coxeter theorem

For every integer `d>=3`, the minimum number of matrix-valued first-order adjacent-transposition Coxeter faces required for universal identification of a bounded unital *-preserving generator on `M_d(C)` modulo Hamiltonian derivations is

`L_d^Cox=floor(d^2/2)`.

#### Proof

The lower bound follows from the quotient dimension `(d^2-1)^2` and the per-face capacity `2d^2`.

For odd dimensions, Theorem 14.1 gives extension-ready minimal designs at the lower-bound count.

For even dimensions, apply Theorem 15.1 to the preceding odd dimension; the face count becomes

`(d-1)^2-1 over 2 + d`

which simplifies to `d^2/2`.

Thus the lower bound is attained for every `d>=3`. QED.

### Remark 16.2 — `d=2`

The scalar-one extension-ready programme has a genuine low-dimensional obstruction in `d=2`: no two-face design attains the embedded restriction ceiling. This does not imply that native two-face tomography in `d=2` is impossible. The sharp theorem above is intentionally stated only for `d>=3`.

## 17. Relation to earlier all-dimensional upper bounds

Earlier constructions proved

`L_d^Cox <= 3d^2-1`

and later

`L_d^Cox <= 2d^2`.

Those constructions remain valid and may be useful because they are more explicit at the transport-family level. They are now historical upper bounds rather than the best face-count theorem.

Likewise, the Weyl-loop construction proving `Theta(d^2)` generalized flat-loop tomography remains an independent structural result.

## 18. Relation to existing literature

The following are standard infrastructure and are not claimed as new:

1. Stinespring dilation;
2. Choi Schwarz inequalities and multiplicative domains;
3. GKSL/Lindblad generator theory;
4. noncommutative carré-du-champ structures;
5. general quantum-channel multiplicative-domain theory;
6. general Lindbladian/Liouvillian tomography and learning.

Existing *holonomy for quantum channels* based on Jamiołkowski/Uhlmann parallel transport is mathematically different from the reduced product defect studied here.

The contribution claimed here is narrower: coupling exact operator-order flatness to UCP multiplicativity loss, deriving the associated exact loop and semigroup identities, and solving the declared finite-dimensional first-order Coxeter-face design problem.

No claim is made that the sharp Coxeter count is a universal lower bound for arbitrary quantum process tomography or arbitrary Lindbladian-learning protocols.

## 20. Claim firewall and open problems

The paper does **not** prove:

- finite-time UCP-channel identifiability from closed loops;
- conditioning bounds or statistical sample complexity;
- robustness under noisy preparation and measurement;
- universal monotonicity of reduced holonomy under further CP reductions;
- equality with entropy production, recoverability, channel-capacity loss, or a standard decoherence monotone;
- infinite-dimensional analogues with unbounded GKSL generators;
- non-Markovian process-tensor generalizations;
- any physical spacetime or gauge-curvature interpretation.

Natural next problems are conditioning-optimal Coxeter design, noisy estimation, operational access restrictions, and domain-controlled infinite-dimensional extensions.

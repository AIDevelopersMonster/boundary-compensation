## 1. Statement

Let `d>=3` be odd and put

`n=d+1`, `N=d+2`.

Assume that a `d`-dimensional minimal Coxeter design is extension-ready. Then there exists an `n`-dimensional minimal Coxeter design with exactly

`L_n=n^2/2`

faces which is itself extension-ready for the block embedding into dimension `N`.

Thus

`ER_d -> ER_(d+1)`

for every odd `d>=3`.

The proof uses exactly `d+1` new faces. One carrier face simultaneously repairs the five-dimensional defect left by the double embedding of the old odd design and removes the matrix bulk of the new diagonal relative variable. The other `d` faces resolve the off-diagonal relative variables and bind their surviving scalar slopes to the three-dimensional relative Hamiltonian gauge.

This theorem is conditional only on the existence of the odd-dimensional extension-ready input design. It does not restore the audited all-odd existence claim by itself.

## 2. Nested restriction algebras

Inside `M_N(C)` consider

`C_d = M_d(C) direct-sum C I_2`

and

`B_n = M_n(C) direct-sum C`.

The complexified normalized restriction quotient for `C_d` has dimension

`R(C_d)=(d^2-1)N^2+5`.

Indeed the complexified output-module decomposition consists of

- one regular `d x d` sector;
- two left modules;
- two right modules;
- one `2 x 2` scalar output block.

For an extension-ready odd `d` design, the double-embedded old rows have rank

`(d^2-1)N^2`.

Hence the old design is short of `R(C_d)` by exactly

`5`.

The defect splits canonically as

`E_5 = E_reg direct-sum (E_sc tensor M_2(C))`,

with

`dim E_reg=1`, `dim E_sc=1`.

Thus the five missing directions are one regular direction plus four copies of one scalar direction.

The restriction quotient for `B_n` has dimension

`R(B_n)=(n^2-1)N^2+2`.

Therefore the relative quotient has dimension

`R(B_n)-R(C_d)=(2d+1)N^2-3`.

The `-3` is exactly the dimension of the relative Hamiltonian gauge described below.

## 3. Relative coordinates

Work modulo `C_d`. For `j=1,...,d`, write

`x_j=E_(j,n)`, `y_j=E_(n,j)`.

A relative map is determined by

`P_j=D(x_j)`, `Q_j=D(y_j)`,

and

`R=D(E_(n,n))=-D(E_(N,N))`.

Before quotienting by derivations this is a space of dimension

`(2d+1)N^2`.

The relative derivations which vanish on `C_d` are generated by the lower `2 x 2` matrix units

`E_(n,n)-E_(N,N)`, `E_(n,N)`, `E_(N,n)`.

Hence the relative Hamiltonian gauge has dimension `3`, and the relative quotient dimension is exactly

`(2d+1)N^2-3`.

## 4. The five old missing directions

Fix representatives

`F in E_reg`, `phi in E_sc`.

They may be chosen normalized, so the regular branch defect is

`Gamma_F(C,D)=F(CD)-F(C)D-CF(D)`,

and the scalar branch defect is

`delta_phi(C,D)=phi(CD)-phi(C)-phi(D)`

in centered coordinates.

Because `F` is a nonzero class modulo derivations, `Gamma_F` is not identically zero. If it vanished on all pairs, `F` would satisfy the Leibniz rule and hence would be an inner derivation on `M_d(C)`, contradicting nontriviality of the class.

Similarly, because `phi` is a nonzero scalar class, `delta_phi` is not identically zero. If it vanished on all pairs, the centered linear map `phi` would be additive under multiplication; expanding at `(I+sX)(I+tY)` forces `phi(XY)=0` for all `X,Y`, hence `phi=0`.

Therefore the loci

`Gamma_F(C,D)!=0`

and

`delta_phi(C,D)!=0`

are nonempty Zariski-open conditions on `GL_d(C)^2`. We may also impose the open condition `det(I-D)!=0`. Thus one may choose `C,D` satisfying all three conditions simultaneously.

## 5. Carrier face

Choose nonzero scalars `u,v` and define in `M_N(C)`

`A=diag(C,u,1)`,

`B=diag(D,v,1)`.

After determinant normalization these are legitimate complexified transports in the embedded `n`-dimensional face family. The inverse branch is `(B^(-1),A^(-1))`.

We first analyze the carrier on the relative variable `R`, taking all `P_j,Q_j=0`.

### Lemma 5.1 — exact carrier operator on `R`

The two branch operators are

`T_+(R)=(uv-1)R-(u-1)RB-(v-1)AR`,

`T_-(R)=(u^(-1)v^(-1)-1)R`

`       -(v^(-1)-1)R A^(-1)`

`       -(u^(-1)-1)B^(-1)R`.

Both annihilate

`E_(n,N)` and `E_(N,n)`.

For a Zariski-open set of `(C,D,u,v)`, their common kernel is exactly

`span{E_(n,N),E_(N,n)}`.

Hence the carrier has relative `R`-rank

`N^2-2`.

#### Proof of generic exactness

For diagonal `C,D`, both operators act entrywise. The two scalar coefficients at a fixed output entry are algebraic functions of the diagonal entries and of `u,v`. The two gauge positions `(n,N)` and `(N,n)` vanish identically. For every other position, simultaneous vanishing imposes a proper algebraic condition. A generic diagonal choice therefore has no further kernel. Nonvanishing of an `(N^2-2)`-minor is then a Zariski-open condition and persists for generic non-diagonal `C,D`. QED.

## 6. The carrier also detects the regular old defect

Set temporarily `v=1` and assume `u!=1`.

On the old `d x d` output block, the relative `R`-image is

`g_+ = (u-1) R(I-D)`,

`g_- = (u^(-1)-1)(I-D^(-1))R`.

Because `I-D` is invertible, the first branch determines `R` uniquely. Therefore an old regular output pair `(h_+,h_-)` lies in the relative image if and only if

`h_- = u^(-1) J_D(h_+)`,

where

`J_D(X)=D^(-1)(I-D) X (I-D)^(-1)`.

The map `J_D` is invertible.

Take

`h_+=Gamma_F(C,D) != 0`,

`h_-=Gamma_F(D^(-1),C^(-1))`.

If `h_-=0`, the pair is never in the relative image. If `h_-!=0`, the equation above can hold for at most one value of `u`, because the right-hand side varies by the scalar factor `u^(-1)` and `J_D(h_+)!=0`.

Hence one may choose `u` so that the regular old defect survives projection to the carrier cokernel.

This survival is an open condition, so it persists after moving `v` from `1` to a nearby generic value with `v!=1`, where Lemma 5.1 gives the full `N^2-2` relative `R`-rank.

Thus the carrier contributes one additional rank direction from `E_reg` beyond its `R`-image.

## 7. The carrier detects all four scalar old directions

Let

`c=phi(C)`, `d0=phi(D)`, `h=phi(CD)`

in centered coordinates, and similarly

`c_-=phi(C^(-1))`, `d_-=phi(D^(-1))`, `h_-=phi(D^(-1)C^(-1))`.

For the scalar defect direction multiplied by a bottom-right matrix unit `K=E_(a,b)`, `a,b in {n,N}`, the two branch outputs are scalar multiples of the same matrix unit:

`s^+_(ab) K`, `s^-_(ab) K`,

where

`s^+_(ab)=h-c b_b-d0 a_a`,

`s^-_(ab)=h_- - d_- (a_a)^(-1) - c_- (b_b)^(-1)`

with

`a_n=u`, `a_N=1`, `b_n=v`, `b_N=1`.

At `u=v=1`, all four first-branch coefficients reduce to

`delta_phi(C,D)`,

which is nonzero by construction. Hence the four scalar old directions form a four-dimensional image for all `(u,v)` in a sufficiently small Zariski-open neighborhood of `(1,1)`.

The relative `R`-image has no component at the two gauge positions `(n,N)` and `(N,n)`, so the two off-diagonal scalar directions are automatically independent of the relative image.

At the diagonal positions the exact relative coefficients are

`r^+_(nn)=-(u-1)(v-1)`,

`r^-_(nn)=-(u-1)(v-1)/(uv)`,

`r^+_(NN)= +(u-1)(v-1)`,

`r^-_(NN)= +(u-1)(v-1)/(uv)`.

Therefore independence of the scalar old direction from the relative `R` direction at the two diagonal positions is equivalent to

`Delta_NN=(u-1)(v-1)[ s^+_(NN)/(uv)-s^-_(NN) ] != 0`,

`Delta_nn=(u-1)(v-1)[ s^-_(nn)-s^+_(nn)/(uv) ] != 0`.

The first bracket is not identically zero as a function of `uv` because `s^+_(NN)=delta_phi(C,D)!=0`.

For the second bracket, after multiplying by `uv`, one obtains the explicit polynomial

`uv h_- + u(d0-c_-) + v(c-d_-) - h`.

Its identically-zero coefficient conditions define a proper algebraic subset of the already nonempty locus `delta_phi(C,D)!=0`; avoid that subset. Then choose `u,v` away from the finitely many zero hypersurfaces above.

Thus the four scalar old directions are independent modulo the relative `R`-image.

## 8. Carrier rank theorem

Combining Sections 5-7 gives:

### Theorem 8.1 — five-defect carrier

There exists one complexified Coxeter square face in dimension `n=d+1` such that, after embedding into dimension `N=d+2`, its measurement on

`E_5 direct-sum M_N(C)_R`

has kernel exactly the two-dimensional relative gauge

`span{E_(n,N),E_(N,n)}`.

Equivalently, the carrier has rank

`(N^2-2)+5=N^2+3`.

Thus the previously isolated `5 x 5` projected determinant is nonzero. More precisely, after entrywise elimination of the `R`-bulk, it factors into

- one nonzero regular projection factor;
- two automatic off-diagonal scalar factors;
- the two diagonal scalar determinants `Delta_nn` and `Delta_NN` above.

No dimension-dependent determinant remains.

## 9. The other `d` faces: local reduction

Choose a multiplicative-Sidon diagonal anchor

`Lambda=diag(lambda_1,...,lambda_d,1,1)`

and, for each `j<=d`, a two-level transport `U_j` mixing only the `j`-th and `n`-th coordinates.

The same entrywise determinant argument as in the local-kernel reduction theorem, now with a two-dimensional scalar tail, reduces the relative variables to

`P_j=alpha_j E_(j,n)+beta_j E_(j,N)`,

`Q_j=gamma_j E_(n,j)+delta_j E_(N,j)`,

while the carrier has already reduced

`R=r E_(n,N)+s E_(N,n)`.

The resulting scalar kernel has dimension `4d+2`.

## 10. First binding scale

Perturb the second transport of the `j`-th local face in the `E_(n,n)` direction. The first nonzero Schur coefficient gives exactly

`beta_j-r=0`,

`delta_j-s=0`.

Thus all far-tail slopes are bound to the two carrier gauge coordinates:

`beta_1=...=beta_d=r`,

`delta_1=...=delta_d=s`.

The residual scalar kernel now has dimension `2d+2`, with variables

`alpha_1,...,alpha_d`, `gamma_1,...,gamma_d`, `r,s`.

## 11. Second binding scale

Next perturb the `j`-th local face by a weaker cross-plane term

`E_(k,n)+E_(n,k)`, `k!=j`.

After bulk elimination and after imposing the first-scale equations, the second-order Schur coefficients are exactly

`alpha_j+gamma_k=0`,

`alpha_k+gamma_j=0`.

Set

`a_j=alpha_j`, `b_j=-gamma_j`.

An edge `{j,k}` therefore imposes

`a_j=b_k`, `a_k=b_j`.

Choose the connected non-bipartite graph consisting of the triangle

`1-2-3-1`

and the edges

`1-j`, `j>=4`.

As in the global-binding lemma, an odd cycle forces the two bipartite value classes to coincide, and connectivity propagates this equality to every vertex. Hence

`alpha_1=...=alpha_d=q`,

`gamma_1=...=gamma_d=-q`.

The only remaining variables are

`q,r,s`,

which are precisely the three relative Hamiltonian gauge parameters.

Therefore the `d` local/binding faces, together with the carrier, resolve the entire relative quotient modulo the three-dimensional gauge.

## 12. Full embedded rank

The old odd extension-ready design has `L_d=(d^2-1)/2` faces.

Add the `d+1` new faces of Sections 5-11. The total number is

`(d^2-1)/2 + d+1`

`=(d+1)^2/2`

`=n^2/2=L_n`,

which is the information-theoretic lower bound for even `n`.

The carrier repairs all five old missing directions and removes the `R`-bulk. The remaining `d` faces resolve the rest of the relative quotient. Hence the double-embedded design attains

`R(B_n)=(n^2-1)N^2+2`,

which is the structural restriction ceiling for even `n`.

Thus the resulting `n`-dimensional design is extension-ready.

Because full restriction-quotient identifiability in `B_n` contains native normalized maps on `M_n(C)` as a subspace, native full rank follows automatically. Therefore the design is both minimal and natively full-rank.

### Theorem 12.1 — conditional odd-to-even extension-ready transfer

For every odd `d>=3`,

`ER_d -> ER_(d+1)`.

The transfer uses exactly `d+1` new Coxeter square faces and preserves the information-theoretic minimum.

## 13. Return to unitary faces

The proof constructs one point in the complexified transport parameter space where a maximal embedded minor is nonzero. Determinant normalization moves the transports into products of `SL_n(C)` without changing rank.

`SU(n)` is Zariski dense in `SL_n(C)`. Hence the same maximal minor is nonzero at some unitary point. The engineered-square realization theorem converts each ordered transport pair into an actual adjacent-transposition Coxeter square.

Therefore Theorem 12.1 holds for genuine unitary Coxeter faces.

## 14. Consequence for the parity programme

The parity transfer picture is now:

- `ER_odd -> ER_even`: proved in this note;
- `ER_even -> minimal_full_odd`: proved by the global binding lemma;
- existence of `ER_odd` in every odd dimension: still the audited open obligation.

Thus the only remaining all-dimensional obstruction is no longer the odd-to-even step. It is the restoration of a rigorous all-odd extension-ready existence theorem, or alternatively a strengthening of the even-to-odd construction so that the resulting odd design is itself extension-ready.

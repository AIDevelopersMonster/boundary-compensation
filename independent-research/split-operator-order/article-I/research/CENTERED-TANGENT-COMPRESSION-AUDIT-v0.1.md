## 1. Purpose

This note audits the finite `H`-anchor compression argument and sharpens the last odd-dimensional tangent barrier.

The earlier note correctly identified the complete `H`-anchor kernel and a binder which reduces its Schur defect by `n(n-2)`. However, the sentence “this is now the only tangent regular barrier” was too strong for two reasons:

1. one must distinguish the **complete binder rank** from the **compressed row-dependency Schur determinant** of a finite `L`-face presentation;
2. even after full embedded regular row rank is obtained, native full rank still requires the unavoidable one-dimensional non-derivation kernel line to tilt out of the native hyperplane `F(I)=0`.

The present note proves the missing finite complete-anchor compression and isolates these two final determinants exactly.

Throughout `n>=3` is odd,

`L=(n^2-1)/2`,

and

`H=diag(h_0,...,h_(n-1))`, `h_j=omega^j`, `omega^n=1`.

## 2. Restricted complete-anchor blocks

For the regular tangent defect

`delta_F(X,Y)=F(XY)-F(X)Y-XF(Y)`,

fix the first anchor `H`.

Choose domain coordinates with `F(H)` separated, then restrict temporarily to maps with

`F(H)=0`.

For one output entry `(a,b)`, the two equations generated by a traceless sample `Y` are represented on the input quotient

`Q=M_n(C)/C H`

by

`T_a(Y)=[(H-h_a I)Y]`,

`R_b(Y)=[Y(H-h_b I)]`.

For an off-diagonal matrix unit `E_(p,q)`,

`T_a(E_(p,q))=(h_p-h_a)E_(p,q)`,

`R_b(E_(p,q))=(h_q-h_b)E_(p,q)`.

### Lemma 2.1 — exact joint block span

For fixed `(a,b)`, the joint span of

`{T_a(Y),R_b(Y): Y in sl_n(C)}`

has dimension

- `n^2-1` if `a=b`;
- `n^2-2` if `a!=b`.

For `a!=b` the unique missing off-diagonal direction is `E_(a,b)`.

#### Proof

If `a!=b`, then

`T_a(E_(a,b))=R_b(E_(a,b))=0`,

so `E_(a,b)` is invisible.

Every other off-diagonal matrix unit is seen by at least one of the two maps. Indeed simultaneous vanishing requires `p=a` and `q=b`.

For the diagonal quotient, restrict to the traceless diagonal subspace `D_0`. The map

`D -> [(H-h_a I)D]`

from `D_0` to the diagonal part of `Q` is injective. If

`(H-h_a I)D=cH`,

the `a`-th diagonal coordinate forces `c=0`; all other diagonal coordinates of `D` vanish, and tracelessness forces the last one to vanish as well. Both spaces have dimension `n-1`, hence the map is an isomorphism.

Thus the diagonal quotient is fully spanned. The only loss for `a!=b` is `E_(a,b)`.

If `a=b`, there is no off-diagonal matrix unit with both coefficients zero, so the entire quotient `Q` is spanned. QED.

## 3. Rank of the complete restricted anchor

Summing the block dimensions over output entries gives

`r_res = n(n^2-1)+n(n-1)(n^2-2)`

`      = n^4-2n^2+n`.

Restoring the `F(H)` columns adds exactly `n` further independent constraints, namely the vanishing of the diagonal of `F(H)`. Therefore

`r_H=n^4-2n^2+2n`,

in agreement with the exact kernel theorem

`dim ker_H=2n(n-1)`.

This calculation also explains why one cannot make all `n^2-1` sampled rows independent in every output block: every off-diagonal output block has one intrinsic missing direction.

## 4. Finite compression of the complete `H` anchor

We now show that exactly

`L=(n^2-1)/2`

traceless samples are enough to realize the whole complete-anchor rowspace.

### Theorem 4.1 — finite complete-anchor compression

There exist traceless matrices

`Y_1,...,Y_L`

such that the `L` paired faces

`(H,Y_i)`, `(Y_i,H)`

have joint regular rank

`n^4-2n^2+2n`.

Equivalently, their kernel is exactly the complete `H`-anchor kernel.

Moreover the set of such `L`-tuples is a nonempty Zariski-open subset of `(sl_n)^L`.

#### Proof

Fix one output block `(a,b)`. By Lemma 2.1 the target joint span has dimension `m_ab`, equal to `n^2-1` on the diagonal blocks and `n^2-2` off the diagonal.

For off-diagonal matrix units, the two coefficients define the projective ratio

`rho_(p,q)=[h_p-h_a : h_q-h_b]`.

A sample of the form

`Y=E_(p,q)+E_(r,s)`

produces two independent off-diagonal directions whenever the two ratios differ.

The diagonal quotient has dimension `n-1`. Choose `n-1` traceless diagonal directions `D_j` whose images under `T_a` span it, and mix each `D_j` with a distinct visible off-diagonal matrix unit. For a generic choice of the mixing coefficients, each such sample contributes one new diagonal pivot and one new off-diagonal pivot.

After these `n-1` mixed samples, all remaining visible off-diagonal directions can be paired by unequal projective ratios. For `a=b` the number remaining is `(n-1)^2`, so exactly `(n-1)^2/2` additional samples suffice. The total is

`(n-1)+(n-1)^2/2=L`.

For `a!=b`, the visible off-diagonal set has one fewer element. After the same `n-1` mixed pivots, the remaining number is `n(n-2)`, which is odd. Use one singleton sample and pair the rest by unequal ratios. The number of additional samples is

`(n(n-2)+1)/2=(n-1)^2/2`,

again giving exactly `L` samples.

The needed pairings exist because a projective ratio fiber has size at most `n`; for `n>=5` no fiber contains more than half of the remaining set, so the standard perfect-matching criterion applies. The case `n=3` is checked directly from the six visible off-diagonal units.

Hence for each fixed `(a,b)` there exists an `L`-tuple realizing the full block span. Nonvanishing of the corresponding block minor is Zariski open. There are finitely many output blocks, so irreducibility of `(sl_n)^L` implies that one common tuple realizes every block maximum simultaneously.

Finally impose the additional open condition that the union of the off-diagonal supports of the samples connects all coordinate labels. Then the diagonal entries of `F(H)` are forced to vanish: an `E_(p,q)` component gives, at output `(p,q)`, the equations `F(H)_(p,p)=0` and `F(H)_(q,q)=0`. Thus the remaining `n` complete-anchor constraints are also present.

Therefore the sampled rank is exactly `r_H`. QED.

## 5. The finite row-dependency space

The `L` sampled faces contain

`2L n^2=(n^2-1)n^2`

scalar regular rows.

Since their rowspace has dimension

`r_H=n^4-2n^2+2n`,

the left-nullspace of the sampled matrix has dimension

`(n^2-1)n^2-r_H`

`=n(n-2)`.

Denote this row-dependency space by

`C_H`.

This dimension exactly matches the rank of the complete-anchor binder on the Schur defect quotient.

## 6. Binder-generic last sample

The complete-anchor kernel is

`K_H=Der_off direct-sum Schur_off`,

with dimension `2n(n-1)`.

Let `S` be the cyclic shift. There exists a traceless matrix `Y_*` such that the pair

`delta(S,Y_*)`, `delta(Y_*,S)`

reduces the Schur multiplier kernel to the path-additive `n`-dimensional family. Hence the complete binder rank on `K_H` is

`2n(n-1)-[n(n-1)+n]`

`=n(n-2)`.

This maximal binder-rank condition is Zariski open in `Y_*`.

The finite complete-anchor compression condition of Theorem 4.1 is also Zariski open in the full `L`-tuple. Therefore one may choose a compressed tuple

`Y_1,...,Y_L`

for which, say, `Y_L` is binder-generic.

This does **not** yet prove the finite perturbative lift: the complete binder rank must still be paired with the actual row dependencies `C_H` of the chosen finite presentation.

## 7. Exact compressed Schur determinant

Perturb only the first anchor of the last face:

`H -> H+tS`.

Let `M(t)` be the resulting regular measurement matrix. At `t=0`,

`rank M(0)=r_H`.

Let

`L_H : C^(2Ln^2) -> C_H`

be any projection onto the left-nullspace of `M(0)`, and let

`Pi_H` denote restriction to the complete-anchor kernel `K_H`.

The first-order compressed Schur operator is

`D_n = L_H [dM/dt]_(t=0) Pi_H`.

Its source quotient has the relevant `n(n-2)`-dimensional Schur defect, and its target is `C_H`, also of dimension `n(n-2)`.

### Final embedded determinant

The remaining embedded regular problem is exactly

`det D_n != 0`.

If this determinant is nonzero, then an `(n^2-1)n^2`-minor of `M(t)` has leading term

`c t^(n(n-2))`, `c!=0`,

and therefore

`rank M(t)=(n^2-1)n^2`

for sufficiently small nonzero `t` outside an algebraic exceptional set.

The earlier complete-binder theorem proves that the uncompressed binder has rank `n(n-2)`. What remains is to show that the row-dependency projection `L_H` does not annihilate any of those `n(n-2)` directions.

This is now a square determinant of size `n(n-2)`, with no hidden matrix-valued bulk.

## 8. Native-rank audit

Even if `det D_n !=0`, one more issue remains before the design is extension-ready.

Full embedded regular row rank leaves a kernel of dimension

`n^4-(n^2-1)n^2=n^2`.

The inner derivations contribute `n^2-1` dimensions, so there is one additional kernel line.

At the complete-anchor/binder tangent limit, that extra line is the cycle-holonomy Schur multiplier. It satisfies

`F(I)=0`.

Therefore, at the exact tangent base point, the native normalized restriction has rank one below the desired value.

For native full rank, the surviving non-derivation kernel line of the **finite perturbed centered family** must tilt out of the native hyperplane

`N={F:F(I)=0}`.

Let `h(t)` be a local generator of the extra kernel line modulo derivations after the embedded rank has been lifted. The required native condition is

`F_I(t)=h(t)(I) != 0`.

Since `F_I(0)=0`, the final native obstruction is a one-dimensional tilt coefficient, for example

`tau_n = d/dt [ell(h(t)(I))]_(t=0)`

for some output functional `ell`, or the first nonzero higher-order coefficient if the linear term vanishes.

Thus the native question has been reduced to one scalar nonvanishing condition.

## 9. Correct final barrier statement

The all-odd extension-ready theorem is now reduced to exactly two finite-dimensional nonvanishing statements:

1. **compressed dependency determinant**

   `det D_n != 0`,

   where `D_n` is `n(n-2) x n(n-2)`;

2. **native tilt scalar**

   `tau_n != 0`

   (or the first nonzero higher-order centered coefficient of the extra kernel line).

All other pieces are theorem-level:

- correct centered scalar-one tangent reduction;
- scalar sector saturation;
- left sector saturation;
- right sector saturation;
- exact complete `H`-anchor kernel;
- exact binder kernel;
- finite compression of the complete `H` anchor into `L` actual faces.

The next proof attack should compute `D_n` in the Schur basis and identify it with an explicit graph/cycle operator. If it factors as the expected incidence/Laplacian form, the first determinant should collapse immediately. After that only the scalar native tilt remains.

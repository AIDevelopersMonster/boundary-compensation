## 1. Transport-restricted multiplicativity defect

Let `Phi:A->B` be UCP and let `S subset A` be a unital set closed under the partial products used by a chosen loop. Define

`mu_Phi(S) := sup { ||Delta_Phi(X,Y)|| : X,Y in S, ||X||<=1, ||Y||<=1 }`.

This is not claimed to be a new global channel metric; it is a transport-restricted defect scale.

### Proposition 1.1 — loop amplification bound

Let `T_1,...,T_m` be unitary transports with

`T_m...T_1=I`,

and assume every `T_k` and every prefix `P_k=T_k...T_1` lies in `S`. Then

`||H_Phi-I|| <= (m-1) mu_Phi(S)`.

#### Proof

Article II gives

`H_Phi-I = - sum_{k=2}^m Phi(T_m)...Phi(T_{k+1}) Delta_Phi(T_k,P_{k-1})`.

UCP maps are contractive, so every prefactor has norm at most one. Taking norms gives the result. QED.

### Interpretation

For a fixed transport family, `mu_Phi(S)` bounds how much loop defect can be generated per multiplication event. A large loop defect therefore requires either appreciable local nonmultiplicativity, a sufficiently long loop, or coherent accumulation of local defects.

## 2. Multiplicative-domain flatness as an exact boundary

### Proposition 2.1 — defect-free transport algebra

Let `C^*(S)` denote the C*-algebra generated by the loop transports. If

`C^*(S) subset MD(Phi)`,

then every reduced loop constructed from `S` remains exactly flat:

`H_Phi=I`.

#### Proof

On the multiplicative domain, `Phi(XY)=Phi(X)Phi(Y)` for all relevant pairs. Hence every term in the exact loop decomposition vanishes. QED.

### Interpretation

The multiplicative domain is therefore an exact algebraic boundary between transport sectors that are preserved without loop defect and sectors on which reduction can generate defect.

This statement is rigorous. The further identification of such sectors with experimentally relevant noiseless or decoherence-free structures is a research target, not part of the proposition.

## 3. Dissipative invisibility on a noise commutant

Let `L` be a bounded GKSL generator

`L(X)=i[H,X] + sum_alpha (V_alpha^* X V_alpha - 1/2{V_alpha^*V_alpha,X})`,

with Leibniz defect

`Gamma_L(X,Y)=sum_alpha [V_alpha^*,X][Y,V_alpha]`.

Define the common noise commutant

`N_V := { X : [X,V_alpha]=[X,V_alpha^*]=0 for every alpha }`.

### Proposition 3.1 — vanishing of the first-order dissipative defect

For all `X,Y in N_V`,

`Gamma_L(X,Y)=0`.

Consequently, if every transport `T_k` and every prefix `P_k` of a flat loop lies in `N_V`, then the first-order reduced loop coefficient vanishes:

`H_t-I = O(t^2)`.

#### Proof

Each commutator in every summand of `Gamma_L(X,Y)` vanishes on `N_V`. Insert this into the first-order loop formula from Article II. QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — first-order loop blindness to dissipatively compatible transport sectors

A Coxeter or other operator loop entirely contained in `N_V` cannot detect the dissipative generator at first order through the Article-II loop coefficient.

### Interpretation

This makes the physical direction precise: first-order loop defects probe **incompatibility** between the chosen transport algebra and the noise operators. The proposition does not claim that this incompatibility is a complete decoherence measure.

## 4. Positive dissipative certificate

### Proposition 4.1 — positive diagonal Leibniz defect

For every `A`,

`Gamma_L(A^*,A)=sum_alpha [A,V_alpha]^*[A,V_alpha] >= 0`.

Moreover,

`Gamma_L(A^*,A)=0`

if and only if

`[A,V_alpha]=0`

for every `alpha` in the chosen GKSL representation.

#### Proof

The first identity is the Article-II GKSL expansion. A finite sum of positive operators vanishes if and only if every summand vanishes. QED.

### Interpretation

For a fixed representation this provides a positive operator certificate of dissipative incompatibility. Any representation-independent operational quantity derived from it would require additional analysis.

## 5. Small-time defect scale

Let `S` be as above and define the generator-restricted scale

`g_L(S):=sup { ||Gamma_L(X,Y)|| : X,Y in S, ||X||<=1, ||Y||<=1 }`.

### Proposition 5.1 — first-order loop estimate

For a fixed `m`-edge flat loop contained in `S`,

`||H_t-I|| <= t (m-1) g_L(S) + O(t^2)`

as `t->0`.

#### Proof

Apply the first-order loop formula

`H_t-I=-t sum_{k=2}^m T_m...T_{k+1} Gamma_L(T_k,P_{k-1})+O(t^2)`

and use unitarity of the exact transports. QED.

### Interpretation

The proposition separates two future questions: algebraic sensitivity (`g_L(S)>0`) and statistical/experimental sensitivity (whether the corresponding coefficient can be estimated stably from finite data).

## 6. An operational target for Article III

For a finite first-order Coxeter design `D`, let `M_D` be the real measurement matrix on the dissipative quotient of dimension

`N_d=(d^2-1)^2`.

Article II solves the algebraic problem

`rank M_D=N_d`

with the sharp face count

`|D|=floor(d^2/2)`.

The next strict layer should use normalized measurement operators. After fixing a physically meaningful normalization convention, define

`sigma_*(D)=sigma_min(M_D)`,

`kappa(D)=sigma_max(M_D)/sigma_min(M_D)`.

### Definition 6.1 — robust Coxeter design

A full-rank design is `epsilon`-robust under the chosen normalization if

`sigma_min(M_D) >= epsilon`.

This definition is intentionally normalization-dependent; the normalization must be fixed before any asymptotic theorem is meaningful.

### Research Problem 6.2 — minimal robust design

Determine

`L_d^rob(epsilon) := min { |D| : sigma_min(M_D)>=epsilon }`

for natural dimension-dependent thresholds `epsilon=epsilon_d`.

This turns the qualitative statement “full rank is not enough” into an explicit mathematical problem.

## 7. Stability dichotomy

The bridge article should not prejudge which of the following regimes is true.

### Conjectural Branch A — robust sharp designs

There exists a normalization and constants `C,p>0` such that for every `d>=3` there is a sharp design with

`|D|=floor(d^2/2)`

and

`kappa(D) <= C d^p`.

If true, algebraic minimality and polynomial stability are compatible.

### Conjectural Branch B — redundancy barrier

There exists a sequence `c_d -> infinity` such that every sharp design satisfies

`kappa(D) >= c_d`,

possibly superpolynomially, while a slightly oversampled family admits much better conditioning.

If true, Article II's sharp count is algebraically optimal but not operationally optimal.

These branches are research alternatives, not simultaneous claims.

## 8. A candidate bridge from multiplicative domain to protected sectors

The following is deliberately stated as a research conjecture rather than a theorem.

### Conjecture 8.1 — protected-algebra correspondence

For physically natural subsystem reductions and Markovian noise models, there exists a useful class of transport algebras `T` for which near-membership in the multiplicative domain of the reduced map is quantitatively equivalent, up to controlled constants and model assumptions, to near-invariance of `T` under the dissipative dynamics.

A successful theorem of this type would connect

`small Delta_Phi on T`

to

`protected/noiseless behaviour of T`.

No such equivalence is claimed in Article II.

## 9. Cohomological language: a disciplined conjectural direction

The exact composition law

`Delta_{Psi o Phi}(X,Y)=Psi(Delta_Phi(X,Y))+Delta_Psi(Phi(X),Phi(Y))`

resembles a chain rule for a defect under composition.

### Research Question 9.1

Can one construct a category or bicategory of reductions in which `Delta` becomes a natural 2-cochain or curvature-like defect, with the composition law realized as a genuine cocycle identity?

A valid positive answer must specify:

1. objects and morphisms;
2. coefficient bimodules or functorial targets;
3. the relevant coboundary operator;
4. the precise sense in which the displayed composition identity is a cocycle law;
5. nontrivial invariants not already contained in ordinary multiplicative-domain theory.

Until these data are supplied, “cohomological” is only a research analogy.

## 10. Geometry gate

### Definition 10.1 — geometry gate for the programme

The programme will regard a future reduced-context construction as a **genuine geometric theory** only if it supplies at least:

1. a space/category of contextual states or configurations;
2. transport maps between them;
3. a composition law;
4. a local connection/defect object;
5. a loop holonomy derived from that local object;
6. a covariance/gauge principle;
7. an operational or representation-independent procedure for evaluating at least one nontrivial geometric quantity.

### Conjecture 10.2 — effective geometry from nonmultiplicative reduction

There exists a nontrivial class of open-system reductions for which the Article-II defect calculus extends to a structure satisfying the geometry gate, and whose loop object reduces to `H_Phi-I` or its infinitesimal analogue in the appropriate limit.

This is the central speculative conjecture of the bridge article. It is not implied by Articles I–II.

## 11. Falsifiability conditions

The programme should regard the geometric/operational direction as unsuccessful if one can prove any sufficiently strong version of the following:

- every sharp or near-sharp Coxeter design is catastrophically ill-conditioned;
- loop coefficients cannot be stably estimated from physically accessible observables without resources comparable to full process tomography;
- the defect calculus yields no representation-independent information beyond standard channel/generator descriptions;
- no useful covariance or local-to-global geometric structure can be built from the defect law;
- non-Markovian extensions destroy the compositional structure required by the geometry gate.

These are not expected results; they are explicit failure criteria.
